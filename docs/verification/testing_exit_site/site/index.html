  • Role of the Gut Microbiota in Nutrition and HealthunknownSourceAuthors: unknown

    This BMJ review argues that the gut microbiota should be treated as a core mediator between diet and health rather than as a side topic of digestion. It links dietary fibre, food additives, drugs, probiotic interventions, and personalised nutrition to measurable changes in microbial metabolites, diversity, and host metabolic outcomes, while stressing that human evidence is still heterogeneous.

  • Microbiome-Directed Nutrition Requires Specific and Personalized InterventionsApr 16, 2026TopicAuthors: unknown

    A second cross-source concept is that microbiome-directed nutrition is modifiable but not generic. The gut-microbiota review argues that diet, medication, and other environmental exposures outweigh host genetics in shaping microbial composition, while both intervention-focused sources emphasize that probiotic, prebiotic, and synbiotic effects depend on the exact strain, substrate, dose, and pairing rather than on broad category labels.

  • Ultra-Processed Dietary Patterns, Microbial Metabolism, and Disease SignalsApr 16, 2026TopicAuthors: unknown

    These sources support a shared topic about ultra-processed dietary patterns as more than a calorie issue. The microbiota review links low-fibre Western-style diets to lower short-chain-fatty-acid production, dysbiosis, and reduced ecosystem resilience, while the US diet study shows ultra-processed foods are the dominant source of added sugars and the cancer cohort associates a higher ultra-processed share with higher overall cancer risk.

  • Effects of Probiotics, Prebiotics, and Synbiotics on Human HealthSep 15, 2017SourceAuthors: unknown

    This 2017 review presents probiotics, prebiotics, and synbiotics as tools for changing gut ecology in ways that may improve human health. Its central message is positive but cautious: benefits appear across multiple conditions, yet they are not generic and depend on the exact strain, substrate, combination, dose, and clinical context.

  • Vitamin D Deficiency Across Europe Is WidespreadFeb 10, 2016SourceAuthors: unknown

    This paper pools VDSP-standardized 25(OH)D data from 18 European population studies to estimate how common vitamin D deficiency is after assay calibration. It finds that deficiency below 30 nmol/L remains widespread, becomes more common in winter, is much higher in some nonwhite subgroups and teenagers, and is highly sensitive to the threshold and standardization choices used in surveillance.

  • Consumption of Ultra-Processed Foods and Cancer RiskunknownSourceAuthors: unknown

    This prospective NutriNet-Sante cohort study links a higher dietary share of ultra-processed foods to higher overall and breast cancer incidence. The association survives several adjustments for nutrient-quality markers, while prostate results are null and colorectal results remain only borderline. The paper argues that processing-related exposures, not only nutrient profile, may matter for cancer risk.

  • Ultra-processed foods as a shared diet-quality and cancer-risk signalApr 16, 2026TopicAuthors: unknown

    Both source records treat ultra-processed food intake as a meaningful exposure pattern rather than a trivial food-category label. The NHANES source links higher intake to a sharply worse added-sugar profile in the US diet, and the NutriNet-Sante source links higher intake to higher overall and breast cancer risk in a large prospective cohort.

  • Ultra-Processed Foods Drive Added Sugars in the US DietunknownSourceAuthors: unknown

    Using NHANES 2009-2010 dietary recall data, the article argues that ultra-processed foods are the main driver of added sugars in the US diet. They supply most added-sugar calories, are far more sugar-dense than less processed foods, and rise in tandem with exceeding the 10% guideline.

  • Self-Adjusting Binary Search Trees1985-07SourceAuthors: Daniel Dominic Sleator, Robert Endre Tarjan

    This paper introduces splay trees, a self-adjusting form of binary search tree that uses splaying rotations instead of explicit balance metadata. It proves amortized logarithmic access and update bounds, derives stronger adaptive guarantees such as static optimality and the working-set bound, and extends the technique to lexicographic and link-cut trees.

  • Lightweight Alternatives to Balanced Search TreesApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: William Pugh, Daniel Dominic Sleator, Robert Endre Tarjan

    Both sources are positioned against the implementation cost of strictly balanced search trees for ordered dictionary workloads. The skip-list paper argues that probabilistic balancing can preserve search-tree-like behavior without explicit balance constraints, while the self-adjusting source presents splay trees as an alternative that also avoids stored balance metadata and instead relies on restructuring during use. The common topic is therefore not balanced trees in general, but lighter-weight ordered-set designs that try to keep logarithmic behavior without traditional balancing machinery.

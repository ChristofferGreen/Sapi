  • Vitamin D Deficiency Across Europe Is WidespreadFeb 10, 2016SourceAuthors: unknown

    This paper pools VDSP-standardized 25(OH)D data from 18 European population studies to estimate how common vitamin D deficiency is after assay calibration. It finds that deficiency below 30 nmol/L remains widespread, becomes more common in winter, is much higher in some nonwhite subgroups and teenagers, and is highly sensitive to the threshold and standardization choices used in surveillance.

  • Consumption of Ultra-Processed Foods and Cancer RiskunknownSourceAuthors: unknown

The association survives several adjustments for nutrient-quality markers, while prostate results are null and colorectal results remain only borderline. The paper argues that processing-related exposures, not only nutrient profile, may matter for cancer risk.

  • Ultra-Processed Foods Drive Added Sugars in the US DietunknownSourceAuthors: unknown

    Using NHANES 2009-2010 dietary recall data, the article argues that ultra-processed foods are the main driver of added sugars in the US diet. They supply most added-sugar calories, are far more sugar-dense than less processed foods, and rise in tandem with exceeding the 10% guideline.

  • Self-Adjusting Binary Search Trees1985-07SourceAuthors: Daniel Dominic Sleator, Robert Endre Tarjan

    This paper introduces splay trees, a self-adjusting form of binary search tree that uses splaying rotations instead of explicit balance metadata. It proves amortized logarithmic access and update bounds, derives stronger adaptive guarantees such as static optimality and the working-set bound, and extends the technique to lexicographic and link-cut trees.

  • Lightweight Alternatives to Balanced Search TreesApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: William Pugh, Daniel Dominic Sleator, Robert Endre Tarjan

    Both sources are positioned against the implementation cost of strictly balanced search trees for ordered dictionary workloads. The skip-list paper argues that probabilistic balancing can preserve search-tree-like behavior without explicit balance constraints, while the self-adjusting source presents splay trees as an alternative that also avoids stored balance metadata and instead relies on restructuring during use. The common topic is therefore not balanced trees in general, but lighter-weight ordered-set designs that try to keep logarithmic behavior without traditional balancing machinery.

  • Skip Lists: A Probabilistic Alternative to Balanced TreesunknownSourceAuthors: William Pugh

    This paper presents skip lists as a randomized replacement for balanced search trees in ordered dictionaries. Random node heights keep search logarithmic in expectation while turning insertion and deletion into local pointer-splice operations. The paper supports that case with probabilistic analysis, parameter tradeoff tables, and benchmark comparisons against AVL, 2-3, and splay trees.

  • Accelerating Photon Mapping for Hardware-Based Ray TracingunknownSourceAuthors: Rene Kern, Felix Brull, Thorsten Grosch

    The paper recasts progressive photon mapping around hardware ray tracing by turning photon collection into inverse radius queries over acceleration structures. Its two new optimizations, Photon Culling and Stochastic Evaluation, reduce wasted storage and shading work in the right scenes, and the evaluation argues that this design reaches useful image quality faster than the compared GPU hash-based alternatives.

  • Hardware-friendly proxy representations for real-time graphicsApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Rene Kern, Felix Brull, Thorsten Grosch, Herman Hansson Soderlund, Alex Evans, Tomas Akenine-Moller, Nelson Max

    Across all three papers, speed comes from replacing unconstrained geometry or light transport with compact proxies that fit GPU execution well. The sphere renderer precomputes motion-blurred appearances in a 2D texture atlas, the SDF-grid paper stores surfaces in sparse voxel structures that RTX traversal can query efficiently, and RTPM turns photon collection into a read-only inverse search over acceleration-structure geometry. The cross-source idea is that representation choice does most of the optimization work before the per-hit shader runs.

  • Scene-dependent approximation controls in GPU renderingApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Rene Kern, Felix Brull, Thorsten Grosch, Herman Hansson Soderlund, Alex Evans, Tomas Akenine-Moller, Nelson Max

    The three papers all use approximations whose value depends on scene conditions rather than treating them as unconditional wins. In RTPM, Photon Culling rejects photons outside camera-relevant hashed cells and Stochastic Evaluation keeps only a tiny reservoir of overlapping photons, but both help most when the scene is large or overlap stays high. The motion-blurred-spheres method similarly switches to a coarser three-rectangle approximation once projected blur exceeds a sphere diameter, and the SDF-grid paper adds blended normals only when the smoother close-up appearance is worth a measurable frame-time increase.

    This source argues that verified compilation can be both formally meaningful and operationally realistic. CompCert is framed as a Clight-to-PowerPC compiler whose machine-checked semantic-preservation proof protects source-level safety arguments, while its architecture, benchmarks, and residual trust-base discussion show what practical verified compilation currently costs and achieves.

  • Conservative reasoning keeps compiler correctness tractableApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Keith D. Cooper, Anshuman Dasgupta

    The graph-coloring allocator and Alive2 both accept conservative imprecision so they can preserve correctness under practical resource limits. CompCert approaches the same broader problem from the proof side: it emphasizes semantic preservation and an explicit trusted base rather than maximal aggressiveness.

  • Deployability matters more than idealized compiler powerApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Keith D. Cooper, Anshuman Dasgupta

    The allocator paper and Alive2 both judge success by useful signal under operational budgets, not by idealized optimality. The CompCert record adds the same deployment stance from another angle: it is positioned as strong assurance engineering rather than as the most aggressive optimizing compiler.

  • Semantic Preservation as the Correctness Contract for Optimizing CompilersApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: unknown

    Alive2 and the CompCert material in this space converge on the same core idea: compiler correctness is better modeled as semantic preservation than as naive behavior-for-behavior equivalence. Alive2 states the LLVM contract as refinement, so an optimized target may remove nondeterminism or undefined behaviors but must not introduce new observable behaviors. The CompCert source record reinforces the same direction by treating the strongest same-observable-behaviors notion as too strict once source semantics are underspecified or optimizations legitimately erase wrong-behavior paths.

  • Ray Tracing of Signed Distance Function GridsunknownSourceAuthors: Herman Hansson Soderlund, Alex Evans, Tomas Akenine-Moller

    This JCGT paper compares ways to ray and path trace signed distance function grids using cubic voxel intersections, sparse traversal structures, and smoother cross-voxel normals. It finds SVS with analytic or Marmitt-style intersection fastest, SBS strongest under tight memory limits, and the shadow-ray optimization broadly worth enabling.

  • Compact approximations for high-performance renderingApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Herman Hansson Soderlund, Alex Evans, Tomas Akenine-Moller, Nelson Max

    Both substantive sources pursue fast rendering by replacing more general image-formation work with compact, prestructured surrogates. The motion-blurred-spheres paper compresses blur extent, lighting rotation, and color handling into a reusable texture-atlas pipeline, while the signed-distance-grid paper compresses geometry into sparse voxel structures and then exploits RTX traversal plus efficient per-voxel intersection tests. The shared concept is not a shared primitive, but a shared design pattern: constrain the representation so expensive work becomes predictable and cheap enough for interactive use.

  • Tailoring Graph-coloring Register Allocation for Runtime CompilationunknownSourceAuthors: Keith D. Cooper, Anshuman Dasgupta

    The paper redesigns Chaitin-Briggs register allocation for just-in-time compilation by avoiding repeated full interference-graph rebuilds. Its lossy allocator stays conservative and safe, cuts allocation cost sharply, and improves total observed JIT runtime on most reported LLVM SPEC integer benchmarks.

  • Compiler Tooling as an Empirical Feedback Loop for Engineering ChoicesApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Keith D. Cooper, Anshuman Dasgupta

    Alive2 and the runtime graph-coloring paper both treat measurement as an input to design, not as an after-the-fact scorecard. Alive2's evaluations exposed concrete refinement failures and clarified where its own limits still block detection. The graph-coloring paper used profiling to isolate the real allocator bottleneck and then judged success on end-to-end runtime rather than on allocator elegance alone.

  • Conservative Approximations That Preserve Safety While Accepting IncompletenessApr 15, 2026TopicAuthors: Keith D. Cooper, Anshuman Dasgupta

    Alive2 and the lossy graph-coloring allocator both make the same engineering bargain: preserve trust by staying conservative, then accept bounded incompleteness or imprecision as the price of deployability. Alive2 aims for zero false alarms by bounding resource use, unrolling loops only to a chosen factor, and over-approximating unsupported features, which means some bugs are intentionally left undetected. The lossy allocator likewise preserves required interference information conservatively even when that adds extra edges and some spill noise.

  • Alive2: Bounded Translation Validation for LLVMunknownSourceAuthors: unknown

    This paper presents Alive2 as a deployable translation validator for LLVM rather than a whole-compiler proof. Its central move is to define optimization correctness by refinement over a semantics that explicitly models undef, poison, memory, and bounded loops. The evaluation shows real impact on LLVM bugs and IR semantics while making the incompleteness boundaries explicit.

  • Efficient Motion-Blurred Spheres Using Texture MappingNov 20, 2024SourceAuthors: Nelson Max

    This paper introduces a compact real-time technique for motion-blurred spheres in WebGL. A precomputed texture atlas, directional-light interpolation, and stretchable long-blur mappings replace more expensive per-frame blur generation. The method is integrated with a hard-sphere chemistry visualization and reaches 60 fps for scenes with up to 400 spheres on laptop hardware.

  • Is Quantum Mechanics Merely a Theory for UsOct 17, 2025SourceAuthors: Peter W. Evans

    Evans argues that preferred-basis selection is constrained by embodied agents rather than fixed absolutely. Brukner's and Pienaar's no-go theorems motivate an agent-centric account in which decoherence yields shared classical facts only for similarly embodied observers. Quantum mechanics is therefore presented as an agent-indexed description of interaction with the world.

  • Quantum ontology without speculationJan 30, 2021SourceAuthors: Matthias Egg

    Egg argues that the ontology of quantum mechanics need not collapse into either speculative interpretation choice or anti-realist restraint. By treating textbook quantum mechanics as a non-fundamental effective theory, he defends realism about spin, collapse, and wave packets at the level where they explain and organize successful practice.

  • Collapse, branches, and wave packets as effective ontologyApr 14, 2026TopicAuthors: Matthias Egg, Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph

    PBR argue that if the quantum state is itself a physical property, measurement forces a hard ontological choice: either collapse is a real physical process or multiple macroscopic branches correspond to reality. Egg argues that textbook quantum mechanics can still treat collapse and wave packets as admissible non-fundamental posits because what matters is their stable functional role in definite recorded outcomes and detector responses. Both sources therefore make measurement structures ontologically serious, even though they characterize that seriousness differently.

  • Quantum-state realism beyond purely informational readingsApr 14, 2026TopicAuthors: Matthias Egg, Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph

    Pusey, Barrett, and Rudolph argue that, given observer-independent physical states and preparation independence, a pure quantum state cannot be treated as mere information about a deeper reality. Egg does not settle the same issue at the fundamental level, but he argues that textbook quantum mechanics can still support real ontology through functional roles. The shared synthesis is that quantum-state discourse carries ontological weight even when the level and metaphysical interpretation of that ontology remain contested.

  • Quantum States Must Track Physical RealityunknownSourceAuthors: Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph

    This paper argues that the quantum state must be closely tied to physical reality, not just to an observer's information, provided independently prepared systems have independent physical states. Its core proof uses forbidden measurement outcomes on jointly measured copies, then strengthens the claim with a noise-tolerant overlap bound that keeps the result experimentally meaningful.

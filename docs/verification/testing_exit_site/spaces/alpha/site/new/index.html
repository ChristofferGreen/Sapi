  • Is Quantum Mechanics Merely a Theory for UsApr 14, 2026SourceAuthors: Peter W. Evans

    Evans argues that preferred-basis selection is constrained by embodied agents rather than fixed absolutely. Brukner's and Pienaar's no-go theorems motivate an agent-centric account in which decoherence yields shared classical facts only for similarly embodied observers. Quantum mechanics is therefore presented as an agent-indexed description of interaction with the world.

  • Quantum ontology without speculationApr 14, 2026SourceAuthors: Matthias Egg

    Egg argues that the ontology of quantum mechanics need not collapse into either speculative interpretation choice or anti-realist restraint. By treating textbook quantum mechanics as a non-fundamental effective theory, he defends realism about spin, collapse, and wave packets at the level where they explain and organize successful practice.

  • Collapse, branches, and wave packets as effective ontologyApr 14, 2026TopicAuthors: Matthias Egg, Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph

    PBR argue that if the quantum state is itself a physical property, measurement forces a hard ontological choice: either collapse is a real physical process or multiple macroscopic branches correspond to reality. Egg argues that textbook quantum mechanics can still treat collapse and wave packets as admissible non-fundamental posits because what matters is their stable functional role in definite recorded outcomes and detector responses. Both sources therefore make measurement structures ontologically serious, even though they characterize that seriousness differently.

  • Quantum-state realism beyond purely informational readingsApr 14, 2026TopicAuthors: Matthias Egg, Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph

    Pusey, Barrett, and Rudolph argue that, given observer-independent physical states and preparation independence, a pure quantum state cannot be treated as mere information about a deeper reality. Egg does not settle the same issue at the fundamental level, but he argues that textbook quantum mechanics can still support real ontology through functional roles. The shared synthesis is that quantum-state discourse carries ontological weight even when the level and metaphysical interpretation of that ontology remain contested.

  • Quantum States Must Track Physical RealityApr 14, 2026SourceAuthors: Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph

    This paper argues that the quantum state must be closely tied to physical reality, not just to an observer's information, provided independently prepared systems have independent physical states. Its core proof uses forbidden measurement outcomes on jointly measured copies, then strengthens the claim with a noise-tolerant overlap bound that keeps the result experimentally meaningful.

Thalamocortical Interaction Time

Evidence Excerpt

The source says conscious sensory experience can take 100–200 milliseconds to develop, low-intensity cortical stimulation may need up to 500 milliseconds to produce a conscious sensation, and visual cortical firing predicts conscious detection after about 80–100 milliseconds. In a model with nine segregated visual areas, functionally specialized neurons in several areas required at least 80 milliseconds to interact constructively and produce a specific correlated firing pattern.

Page references: pp.17-18

Type: model_constraint

This evidence gives a temporal constraint on information integration. It supports the claim that consciousness is not tied to the first local response to a stimulus but to the later period when effective interactions among distributed cortical elements can build. The evidence combines psychophysics, stimulation, recordings, and large-scale modeling, so it is stronger as converging timing support than as a single direct measurement of Φ.

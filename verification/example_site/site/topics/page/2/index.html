  • Formal Structure of Grounding RelationsApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 9Authors: Fabrice Correia, Alexander Skiles, Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra

    The generalized-identity source offers formal analyses of grounding through disjunctive parthood, quasi-subsumption, and preservation under factual identity. The strict-order source instead challenges familiar structural assumptions by arguing that grounding is not transitive, irreflexive, or asymmetric.

  • Grounding, Essence, and NecessitationApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 9Authors: Fabrice Correia, Alexander Skiles, Kelly Trogdon

    One source argues that full grounding metaphysically necessitates what is grounded by appeal to cognitive significance and Fine-style essence, while the generalized-identity source explains grounding-to-necessitation patterns through what it is to be the grounded property or fact. The shared topic is not merely that grounding entails necessity, but that essence-like structure is used to explain why the entailment is metaphysically secure.

  • Truthmaking, Facts, and Non-Factive IdentityApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 6Authors: Fabrice Correia, Alexander Skiles, Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra

    The truthmaking source treats truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding as species of grounding, including cases where truthmakers ground truth facts. The generalized-identity source supplies a non-factive no-difference operator that can apply to factual and generic contents without taking fact-shaped or property-shaped entities as relata.

  • Mindfulness as Multidimensional Mental TrainingApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 10Authors: David R. Vago, David A. Silbersweig, Nicholas T. Van Dam, ...

    S-ART frames mindfulness as training across self-awareness, self-regulation, and self-transcendence rather than as a single capacity. This aligns with the critical claim that mindfulness is an umbrella term whose studies must specify the targeted practices, faculties, interventions, and outcomes.

    S-ART distinguishes experiential enactive, experiential phenomenological, and narrative self-processing systems and proposes that mindfulness can reduce biased narrative self-processing through regulation and decentering.

  • Mindfulness, attention, and mechanism-level caveatsApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 10Authors: Nicholas T. Van Dam, Marieke K. van Vugt, David R. Vago, ...

    Mindfulness training evidence points to changes in specific attentional subsystems rather than a single global attention benefit. The attention-training source reports improved orienting after an 8-week MBSR course, altered alerting after a 1-month intensive retreat among experienced meditators, and baseline conflict-monitoring differences in participants with prior concentrative practice.

  • Operationalizing mental constructs across consciousness, attention, and mindfulness researchApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 10Authors: Nicholas T. Van Dam, Marieke K. van Vugt, David R. Vago, ...

    The sources share a concern with turning broad mental concepts into measurable structures. Integrated information theory defines consciousness through complexes and phi, attention studies separate alerting, orienting, conflict monitoring, and sensory suppression, while mindfulness research warns against treating mindfulness as a unitary or universally defined construct.

  • Rigor, safety, and evidence limits in mindfulness claimsApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 10Authors: Nicholas T. Van Dam, Marieke K. van Vugt, David R. Vago, ...

    The mindfulness critique argues that clinical, media, educational, corporate, and military enthusiasm has outpaced an uneven evidence base. Preliminary clinical trials, condition-specific benefits, under-assessed adverse effects, opportunity costs, and cautious interpretation of neuroimaging all limit broad implementation claims.

  • Attentional Gating and Sensory SuppressionApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 14Authors: John J. Foxe, Adam C. Snyder, Amishi P. Jha, ...

    Selective attention can be framed as a family of gating mechanisms that improve behavior by prioritizing relevant inputs and suppressing distracting ones. The alpha-oscillation source emphasizes neural suppression of irrelevant sensory processing, while the mindfulness source distinguishes behavioral attention subsystems that can change with training.

  • Cortical Network Organization and Conscious AccessApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 8Authors: John J. Foxe, Adam C. Snyder, Giulio Tononi

    Across the sources, cortical organization matters both for conscious experience and for selective attention, but the claims operate at different explanatory levels. Information integration theory addresses the conditions for conscious experience, while alpha-band attention research addresses suppressive modulation of sensory processing.

  • Differentiated and Integrated Mental FunctionsApr 26, 2026TopicEvidence: 0Claims: 13Authors: Giulio Tononi, Amishi P. Jha, Jason Krompinger, ...

    This topic should be framed as a cautious bridge between two levels of analysis. Tononi treats consciousness as a system-level capacity for differentiated-yet-unified information integration, while Jha, Krompinger, and Baime treat attention as separable alerting, orienting, and conflict-monitoring subsystems rather than one global attention capacity.

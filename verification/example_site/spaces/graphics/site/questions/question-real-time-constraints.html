What tradeoffs matter most in real-time rendering systems?

Active question | 3 sources, 14 claims, 11 evidence items, 10 measurements

Current synthesis

Current evidence supports a performance-quality-validity framing: real-time rendering systems gain budget by reusing, resampling, or adaptively allocating work, but the important tradeoffs are whether reused data remains valid, whether bias or staleness is visually acceptable, whether validation and refresh costs erase the savings, and whether perceptual quality is better served by resolution, refresh rate, or sampling quality under the current motion and scene conditions.

Conclusions

  • Temporal coherence is a strong general optimization lever because adjacent frames often let systems reuse intermediate or final shading work, but the best targets are expensive computations whose values remain stable under expected camera, lighting, time, and interaction changes. (strong support)
  • Cache refresh and validation are first-order design costs: cached values become stale, and refresh policies trade speed against noise, discontinuities, branch costs, and visible resampling or shading errors. (strong support)
  • Measured temporal-coherence examples show that the payoff can be large but content-dependent: reported speedups include 2.1x for reflection-shader caching, 8x for static ambient-occlusion caching, and 15x or larger for broader ambient-occlusion reuse, with the broader reuse case producing visible errors. (strong support)
  • For many-light direct illumination, ReSTIR evidence supports spatiotemporal reservoir reuse as a high-impact real-time strategy: the allowed evidence reports 6x-60x equal-error speedups for the unbiased estimator, 35x-65x for the biased estimator, and 1920 x 1080 scene timings of 15 ms with 11,000 dynamic emissive triangles and 50 ms with 3.4 million dynamic emissive triangles. (strong support)
  • The ReSTIR evidence also shows the central bias-cost tradeoff: biased spatiotemporal reuse can lower error at interactive budgets, while unbiased reuse requires corrected candidate-PDF weighting and can impose extra visibility-aware sampling cost. (moderate support)
  • Reuse methods are broad but not universal: temporal coherence spans many real-time rendering tasks, while image-space reservoir reuse is limited by spatial and temporal correlation, disocclusions, fast lighting changes, high geometric complexity, and the demonstrated first-hit direct-lighting scope. (moderate support)
  • Perceptual constraints add another tradeoff layer: adaptive rendering quality depends jointly on spatial resolution, refresh rate, object velocity, and motion predictability, with blur and judder components explaining why the preferred allocation changes with motion. (strong support)
  • Model-driven adaptive control can improve perceived quality under a fixed budget: MARRR uses the perceptual model to choose refresh rate and resolution in real time, and validation evidence reports overall preference for MARRR over fixed-rate baselines, with stronger results for predictable user-induced motion. (strong support)

Uncertainty

The evidence gives strong qualitative guidance and several source-specific measurements, but it does not produce a universal frame-time or quality rule. Applicability depends on hardware, scene dynamics, display behavior, target error metric, denoising pipeline, refresh and validation policy, disocclusion behavior, lighting changes, motion predictability, and whether the task fits image-space or temporal-reuse assumptions.

Ten-Thousand Avatar Benchmark

Evidence Excerpt

For the results in Figures 8 and 9, we displayed 2,000 avatars for each of four types of humans and 1,000 for each of the two last type, thus displaying 10,000 different humans. One of these types is a jogger, thus having an animation different from the others. These humans move in a village modeled with 41,260 polygons. The display updates between 12 and 20 frames per second, depending on the displayed polygonal complexity. To evaluate our simulation’s scalability, we simulated different sized crowds. The frame per second rate for 0 people was 26.20; for 1,000 people, it was 24.08; for 5,000 people, it was 18.26; and for 10,000 people, it was 13.35.

Page references: pp.41-42, Fig.8, Fig.9, Fig.10

Type: measurement

The benchmark is the clearest empirical evidence for the rendering pipeline. It gives the hardware-independent reader concrete crowd sizes, scene complexity, and frame-rate measurements, while also showing that the baseline city model itself consumes substantial rendering resources.

ReSTIR Makes Dynamic Direct Lighting Real-Time

ReSTIR treats real-time many-light direct illumination as a reservoir resampling problem. It stores compact per-pixel reservoirs, reuses selected candidates across neighbors and prior frames, and offers biased and unbiased modes. The result is fast GPU rendering with clear limits around discontinuities, weak correlation, and first-hit image-space scope.

Overview and Commentary

This paper introduces ReSTIR, Reservoir-based Spatio-Temporal Importance Resampling, for real-time ray-traced direct lighting with many dynamic emitters. The core problem is that a renderer cannot trace shadow rays to all lights, and in real-time settings it may only afford a few rays per pixel. ReSTIR addresses that constraint by generating a small set of per-pixel light candidates, resampling them with a target distribution based on unshadowed path contribution, and storing only the selected sample, its weight, and the effective candidate count in a reservoir. The key algorithmic move is that reservoirs can be combined as if their original candidate streams had been concatenated, which lets nearby pixels and prior frames share useful sampling information without storing every candidate. This turns spatial and temporal coherence into better sampling PDFs inside the renderer rather than post-process filtering of colors. The paper also analyzes why naive reuse across pixels can introduce bias: neighboring pixels may have different target distributions, visibility, normals, and supports. It derives an unbiased correction using modified RIS weights and multiple-importance-style candidate-PDF weights, but notes that visibility-aware unbiased reuse can require additional shadow rays. As a result, the implementation offers both biased and unbiased variants. The biased version is faster and often lower variance at interactive budgets, but it can darken difficult regions such as shadow boundaries, highlights, or complex geometry. The reported results are substantial: the abstract claims equal-error speedups of 6x-60x for the unbiased estimator and 35x-65x for the biased estimator, and figures show scenes with 11,000 to 3.4 million dynamic emissive triangles rendered at 1920 x 1080 in tens of milliseconds. The method is strongest as a first-hit direct-lighting technique in scenes where neighboring pixels and previous frames provide relevant samples. It is weaker near disocclusions, fast moving lights, lighting discontinuities, and cases where image-space correlation breaks down; extending it beyond primary-hit direct lighting remains future work.

Section 01

Problem Setting

The source targets one-bounce direct lighting from many dynamic emissive surfaces under real-time ray budgets. It treats the problem as hard even for offline renderers because important lights depend jointly on visibility, BSDF response, geometry, and emitted power, while interactive applications cannot afford exhaustive shadow rays or heavy per-frame structures.

Section 04

Performance Evidence

The reported measurements connect the math to GPU rendering. The abstract gives equal-error speedups of 6x-60x for the unbiased estimator and 35x-65x for the biased estimator, while figure captions report real-time images at 1920 x 1080 with 11,000 and 4 million dynamic emissive triangles.

Section 05

Denoising Interpretation

ReSTIR is positioned as complementary to denoising rather than a replacement for it. The paper argues that denoisers cannot restore features missing from their input samples, so improving sample quality inside the renderer can make the denoiser's later reconstruction task better conditioned.

Section 06

Assumptions And Limits

The approach relies on spatial and temporal sample relevance. It is less effective around disocclusions, lighting discontinuities, fast moving lights, and high geometric complexity. Because the implementation stores reservoirs in image buffers, its demonstrated scope is primary-hit direct lighting rather than arbitrary deeper-path global illumination.

Claims Referencing This Source

  • MIS preserves unbiased reuse

    A modified RIS weight using candidate-PDF multiple importance weights can remove bias introduced by mixing samples from different pixel distributions, though visibility-aware unbiased reuse costs extra rays.

  • Better samples improve denoising

    Improving the quality of direct-light samples before denoising can make existing denoisers more effective because denoisers cannot reconstruct image features absent from their input samples.

  • Many lights challenge real-time

    Many-light direct illumination remains difficult in real time because only a small number of rays can be traced per pixel while important lights depend on visibility, material response, geometry, and emitter power.

  • Reservoirs amplify candidate counts

    Reservoir sampling makes spatiotemporal reuse practical because a pixel can combine selected samples and weight sums from neighboring reservoirs without storing each candidate stream.

  • ReSTIR accelerates equal-error rendering

    ReSTIR achieves substantially faster equal-error rendering than the compared state-of-the-art many-light method, including reported 6x-60x speedups for the unbiased estimator and 35x-65x for the biased estimator.

  • ReSTIR reuses light candidates

    ReSTIR samples one-bounce direct lighting by repeatedly resampling light candidates and reusing useful samples across spatial neighbors and prior frames.

  • Biased reuse darkens discontinuities

    The biased ReSTIR estimator lowers variance and improves visual quality at interactive render times, but it can lose energy and darken regions near geometric, visibility, or shading discontinuities.

  • Image-space reuse limits scope

    The method depends on useful spatial or temporal correlation and on image-space reservoirs, so performance degrades near disocclusions, fast lighting changes, high geometric complexity, and beyond first-hit direct lighting.

  • Two passes avoid hierarchies

    A two-pass accumulation-field method fits directly into the OpenGL pipeline and avoids extra point-hierarchy storage for node aggregation.

  • Taxonomy has three dimensions

    A useful taxonomy for foveated rendering classifies methods by required input data type, foveation principle, and rendering paradigm.

  • Refresh controls cache staleness

    Cached temporal data must be refreshed because reused values become stale through shading changes and resampling error, so refresh strategies trade coherence, noise, discontinuities, and GPU branch costs.

  • VR latency causes discomfort

    Complex VR scenes exceed the efficient real-time rendering capacity of current devices, and delayed rendering can make displayed content inconsistent with user interaction, causing discomfort.

  • CUDA accelerates skeleton bundling

    CUDA image-space distance transforms, skeletonization, path computation, and attraction make the skeleton-based edge bundling pipeline substantially faster than a CPU AFMM implementation on the tested graphs.

  • Disocclusion requires hole filling

    Disoccluded regions constrain temporal reuse because previous-frame data may be absent; practical systems must reshade these regions or fill holes approximately.

  • Forward reprojection trades geometry

    Forward reprojection can map cached pixels directly into the target frame without processing current scene geometry, but it depends on motion vectors and must address scattering, occlusion, and holes.

  • Foveation preserves perceived quality

    Foveated rendering can accelerate rendering by giving the foveal region high quality and the peripheral region lower quality while preserving perceived visual quality.

  • Foveation requires three decisions

    Foveated rendering research is organized around three recurring problems: applying human-visual-system perceptual models, rendering regions at different qualities, and integrating foveation into rendering paradigms.

  • Open problems remain broad

    Future foveated-rendering work needs broader use of human visual features, improved gaze and AI techniques, better methods for new data types, more suitable rendering paradigms, stronger objective quality metrics, and deeper AR and task-oriented applications.

  • PCA planes reduce popping

    Perturbing an impostor plane toward a PCA-derived best-fitting plane reduces viewpoint-change popping for walking human impostors while limiting newly introduced distortions.

  • Seed points preserve density

    Pixel-bin seed points reduce node-density rendering work without changing the density field produced for a given viewport.

  • Frames preserve visible points

    Real-time rendering sequences exhibit substantial spatio-temporal coherence, and game-like scenes often keep more than 90 percent of visible surface points visible from one frame to the next.

  • Resampling accumulates blur

    Repeated reprojection resampling can attenuate and misplace high frequencies, so temporal reuse needs periodic recomputation, better filtering, or fading of older frames to avoid accumulated blur.

  • Reverse reprojection validates reuse

    Reverse reprojection caching validates reuse by mapping a current pixel to its previous-frame position and accepting the cached value only when depth or identity tests indicate the same surface point.

  • Bundling trades detail for structure

    Skeleton-based edge bundling is most appropriate for coarse-scale connectivity exploration because bundling overdraws individual edges and distorts edge directions.

  • Smoothing controls bundle structure

    Smoothing, relaxation, and cushion shading let users adjust whether bundles appear as sharp organic branches or smoother force-directed-like curves while preserving the computed connectivity pattern.

  • LOD renders large graphs

    Straight-line graph drawings can be rendered interactively with adjustable level of detail by combining density-based node aggregation with edge cumulation on graphics hardware.

  • Coherence amortizes shading work

    Temporal coherence can reduce rendering cost by reusing intermediate or final shading information from previous frames instead of recomputing every frame from scratch.

  • Image space avoids preprocessing

    The edge aggregation approach operates in image space and does not require precomputed supporting data structures such as hierarchies or meshes.

  • Full model fits best

    The full model fits measured motion-quality curves better when it includes parallel blur, judder, and separate handling of predictable and unpredictable motion.

  • MARRR improves adaptive rendering

    The MARRR algorithm uses the perceptual model in real time to choose refresh rate and resolution under a rendering budget, and validation experiments generally prefer it over constant-rate baselines.

  • HVS models guide foveation

    Visual acuity models and contrast sensitivity models are the two representative perceptual-model families used to guide foveated rendering quality allocation.

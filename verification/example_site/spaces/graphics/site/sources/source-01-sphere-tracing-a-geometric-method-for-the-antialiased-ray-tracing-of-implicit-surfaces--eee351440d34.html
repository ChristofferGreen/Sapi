Claims Referencing This Source

  • Shaders direct GPU rendering

    A shader is a GPU-directed program that describes rendering processes for turning 3D spatial data and physical properties into an image.

  • Shadercraft requires mathematical fluency

    Creating original shaders requires substantial mathematical and technical fluency in functions, vectors, matrices, and spatial reasoning.

  • Digital art bridges calculation

    Digital art practice must negotiate the tension between scientific-technical processes and the desire to produce sensible artistic experience.

  • Fragment shaders generate patterns

    Fragment shaders map two-dimensional pixel positions to colors and can generate animated mathematical patterns responsive to time or external inputs.

  • Mastery enables live improvisation

    Mastered shadercoding can support real-time artistic improvisation that fuses code, image, performer, and audience into a live digital performance.

  • Constraints drive shader creativity

    Mathematical and technical constraints in shadercoding can become creative motors rather than merely limiting conditions.

  • Raymarching recreates shader depth

    Raymarching can recreate three-dimensional depth inside a fragment shader by marching a ray through a distance-field description until it reaches an object.

  • Showdowns stage shader performance

    Shader Showdowns turn shadercoding into a timed public performance evaluated through the final image, code technique, and visual evolution during the event.

  • Shadercoding blends code and art

    Shadercoding blends programming, mathematics, and graphics into a digital-art practice that can be theorized technically and performatively.

  • Grid tiles guide motion

    A regular grid of environment tiles can support both collision behavior and shadowing decisions for moving virtual humans.

  • Two passes avoid hierarchies

    A two-pass accumulation-field method fits directly into the OpenGL pipeline and avoids extra point-hierarchy storage for node aggregation.

  • Taxonomy has three dimensions

    A useful taxonomy for foveated rendering classifies methods by required input data type, foveation principle, and rendering paradigm.

  • Multipass coloring diversifies avatars

    Alpha-channel multipass rendering can recolor significant body regions such as clothes, hair, and skin to increase avatar variety while preserving baked texture shading.

  • VR latency causes discomfort

    Complex VR scenes exceed the efficient real-time rendering capacity of current devices, and delayed rendering can make displayed content inconsistent with user interaction, causing discomfort.

  • CUDA accelerates skeleton bundling

    CUDA image-space distance transforms, skeletonization, path computation, and attraction make the skeleton-based edge bundling pipeline substantially faster than a CPU AFMM implementation on the tested graphs.

  • Hill climbing aggregates edges

  • Foveation preserves perceived quality

    Foveated rendering can accelerate rendering by giving the foveal region high quality and the peripheral region lower quality while preserving perceived visual quality.

  • Foveation spans multiple principles

  • Open problems remain broad

    Future foveated-rendering work needs broader use of human visual features, improved gaze and AI techniques, better methods for new data types, more suitable rendering paradigms, stronger objective quality metrics, and deeper AR and task-oriented applications.

  • Sampling clusters similar edges

    Hierarchical agglomerative clustering with full linkage and Euclidean distances between uniformly sampled edge points yields clusters of geometrically close edges that follow graph structure.

  • Impostors enable real-time crowds

    Image-based impostor rendering with positional and directional discretization can display dense animated crowds in large virtual urban environments at interactive rates.

  • Compressed textures enable variety

    OpenGL compressed textures and compact frame storage allow ten human types with ten animation frames each to fit in about 25 Mbytes of texture memory.

  • Sample packing cuts memory

    Packing each prerendered human sample into the smallest containing rectangle reduces the texture memory needed for human images to one quarter of the original value without loss of image quality.

  • PCA planes reduce popping

    Perturbing an impostor plane toward a PCA-derived best-fitting plane reduces viewpoint-change popping for walking human impostors while limiting newly introduced distortions.

  • Seed points preserve density

    Pixel-bin seed points reduce node-density rendering work without changing the density field produced for a given viewport.

  • Iterative reclustering coarsens bundles

    Repeated reclustering with decreasing similarity thresholds creates bottom-up bundling in which fine local bundles merge into coarser structures.

  • Path regularization prevents kinks

    Restricting attraction to skeleton paths passing through endpoint feature points prevents sharp kinks caused by feature-transform singularities.

  • Bundling trades detail for structure

  • Coherence amortizes shading work

    The attraction rule moves interior edge points toward feature-transform locations on the skeleton while keeping edge endpoints fixed at their node positions.

  • Impostors project human shadows

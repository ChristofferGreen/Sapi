Temporal Stability, Artifacts, and Frame-to-Frame Reuse

Structure: wiki

Section 1

A concept page could connect perceptual motion artifacts with the technical limits of reusing frame data. The motion-quality evidence identifies refresh-rate thresholds, velocity effects, judder, and predictability; temporal-coherence techniques explain how reprojection, cached values, disocclusion, and repeated resampling can create blur, holes, lag, or stale shading.

Section 2

The synthesis is that real-time rendering systems must manage both measured human sensitivity to motion and the numerical artifacts of temporal reuse. Support is cross-source but not identical: the motion-quality paper measures perceived artifacts, while the temporal-coherence survey explains rendering mechanisms that can produce or mitigate temporal artifacts.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • Amortization trades variance lag

    Amortized sampling uses a reprojected running average to reduce sampling variance, but the smoothing factor creates a trade-off between noise reduction and temporal lag or blur.

  • Disocclusion requires hole filling

    Disoccluded regions constrain temporal reuse because previous-frame data may be absent; practical systems must reshade these regions or fill holes approximately.

  • Predictability affects pursuit accuracy

    Eye-tracking data show that smooth pursuit is not affected by refresh rates above 27.5 Hz but is strongly affected by whether motion is predictable or unpredictable.

  • Velocity lowers perceived quality

    Higher object velocities reduce perceived quality at a fixed refresh rate because they increase motion blur and make content harder to inspect.

  • Judder fades above 72Hz

    Judder is detectable at 50 Hz and 60 Hz but becomes negligible at 72 Hz and higher refresh rates in the isolated-judder experiment.

  • Resampling accumulates blur

    Repeated reprojection resampling can attenuate and misplace high frequencies, so temporal reuse needs periodic recomputation, better filtering, or fading of older frames to avoid accumulated blur.

  • Refresh gains diminish above 100Hz

    Subjective motion-quality measurements from 50 Hz to 165 Hz show that higher refresh rates improve perceived quality, but gains become harder to observe above roughly 100 Hz.

  • Coherence amortizes shading work

    Temporal coherence can reduce rendering cost by reusing intermediate or final shading information from previous frames instead of recomputing every frame from scratch.

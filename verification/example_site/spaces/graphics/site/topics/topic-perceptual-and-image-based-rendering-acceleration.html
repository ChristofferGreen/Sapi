Perceptual and image-based strategies for real-time rendering acceleration

Structure: wiki

Section 1

Foveated rendering and image-based impostor rendering both respond to real-time rendering pressure by reducing work where full geometric or full-quality rendering is not necessary. Foveated rendering allocates high quality to the foveal region and lower quality to peripheral regions, while crowd impostors replace complex human models with optimized image samples to keep dense virtual environments interactive.

Section 2

The shared concept is selective fidelity: rendering systems can preserve the experience that matters to the viewer while changing the underlying representation, quality, or detail level. The foveated-rendering survey frames this around human-visual-system models and regional quality variation; the crowd-rendering system frames it around positional and directional discretization, texture compression, and impostors.

Section 3

This topic should also note the limits of the comparison. Foveated rendering is explicitly perceptual and spans many rendering paradigms, whereas the crowd-rendering source focuses on a concrete image-based technique for animated humans in virtual cities. Both nevertheless treat rendering cost as a central design constraint for interactive visual systems.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • VR latency causes discomfort

    Complex VR scenes exceed the efficient real-time rendering capacity of current devices, and delayed rendering can make displayed content inconsistent with user interaction, causing discomfort.

  • Foveation preserves perceived quality

    Foveated rendering can accelerate rendering by giving the foveal region high quality and the peripheral region lower quality while preserving perceived visual quality.

  • Foveation requires three decisions

    Foveated rendering research is organized around three recurring problems: applying human-visual-system perceptual models, rendering regions at different qualities, and integrating foveation into rendering paradigms.

  • Impostors enable real-time crowds

    Image-based impostor rendering with positional and directional discretization can display dense animated crowds in large virtual urban environments at interactive rates.

  • Compressed textures enable variety

    OpenGL compressed textures and compact frame storage allow ten human types with ten animation frames each to fit in about 25 Mbytes of texture memory.

  • Sample packing cuts memory

    Packing each prerendered human sample into the smallest containing rectangle reduces the texture memory needed for human images to one quarter of the original value without loss of image quality.

  • Ten thousand humans remain interactive

    The tested system renders 10,000 different humans in a 41,260-polygon village at 12 to 20 frames per second, with measured frame rates falling from 26.20 fps at zero people to 13.35 fps at 10,000 people.

  • HVS models guide foveation

    Visual acuity models and contrast sensitivity models are the two representative perceptual-model families used to guide foveated rendering quality allocation.

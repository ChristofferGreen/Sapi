Sample Quality and Perceptual Quality Allocation

Structure: wiki

Boundaries

The comparison should remain bounded: ReSTIR's evidence concerns light-sample selection and estimator behavior, while foveated rendering's evidence concerns human-visual-system models and regional quality variation.

Quality budgets

Several sources treat real-time rendering as a problem of spending limited computation where it matters most. ReSTIR improves many-light direct illumination by raising direct-light sample quality before denoising, while foveated rendering allocates high quality to the foveal region and lower quality to the periphery.

Shared synthesis

The evidence supports a synthesis around quality budgets: statistical estimators, perceptual models, and rendering paradigms all choose where approximation is acceptable and where missing detail, latency, or discontinuities become visible.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • VR latency causes discomfort

    Complex VR scenes exceed the efficient real-time rendering capacity of current devices, and delayed rendering can make displayed content inconsistent with user interaction, causing discomfort.

  • Foveation preserves perceived quality

    Foveated rendering can accelerate rendering by giving the foveal region high quality and the peripheral region lower quality while preserving perceived visual quality.

  • Foveation requires three decisions

    Foveated rendering research is organized around three recurring problems: applying human-visual-system perceptual models, rendering regions at different qualities, and integrating foveation into rendering paradigms.

  • Better samples improve denoising

    Improving the quality of direct-light samples before denoising can make existing denoisers more effective because denoisers cannot reconstruct image features absent from their input samples.

  • Many lights challenge real-time

    Many-light direct illumination remains difficult in real time because only a small number of rays can be traced per pixel while important lights depend on visibility, material response, geometry, and emitter power.

  • ReSTIR accelerates equal-error rendering

    ReSTIR achieves substantially faster equal-error rendering than the compared state-of-the-art many-light method, including reported 6x-60x speedups for the unbiased estimator and 35x-65x for the biased estimator.

  • HVS models guide foveation

    Visual acuity models and contrast sensitivity models are the two representative perceptual-model families used to guide foveated rendering quality allocation.

Model-Driven Real-Time Rendering Tradeoffs

Structure: wiki

Section 1

A concept page could compare rendering systems that use explicit models to choose practical approximations under real-time constraints. The motion-quality work uses a fitted perceptual model for refresh-rate and resolution selection; ReSTIR uses reservoir sampling and biased or unbiased estimators to trade variance, cost, and correctness; foveated rendering uses perceptual models and taxonomies to organize quality allocation across rendering paradigms.

Section 2

The common concept is controlled approximation: each method accelerates rendering by modeling which samples, pixels, regions, or display states matter most. The sources also show that model-driven choices have failure modes, such as poor motion predictability, bias near discontinuities, weak spatiotemporal correlation, or incomplete perceptual metrics.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • Taxonomy has three dimensions

    A useful taxonomy for foveated rendering classifies methods by required input data type, foveation principle, and rendering paradigm.

  • Open problems remain broad

    Future foveated-rendering work needs broader use of human visual features, improved gaze and AI techniques, better methods for new data types, more suitable rendering paradigms, stronger objective quality metrics,...

  • Reservoirs amplify candidate counts

    Reservoir sampling makes spatiotemporal reuse practical because a pixel can combine selected samples and weight sums from neighboring reservoirs without storing each candidate stream.

  • ReSTIR reuses light candidates

    ReSTIR samples one-bounce direct lighting by repeatedly resampling light candidates and reusing useful samples across spatial neighbors and prior frames.

  • Biased reuse darkens discontinuities

    The biased ReSTIR estimator lowers variance and improves visual quality at interactive render times, but it can lose energy and darken regions near geometric, visibility, or shading discontinuities.

  • Full model fits best

    The full model fits measured motion-quality curves better when it includes parallel blur, judder, and separate handling of predictable and unpredictable motion.

  • MARRR improves adaptive rendering

    The MARRR algorithm uses the perceptual model in real time to choose refresh rate and resolution under a rendering budget, and validation experiments generally prefer it over constant-rate baselines.

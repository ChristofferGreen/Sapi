Real-time graphics as a performance medium

Structure: wiki

Domain Boundary

A topic page should keep the domains distinct: one source concerns artistic live coding, while the other concerns rendering-system engineering, but both rely on technical control to sustain real-time visual experience.

Performance Evidence

The shared concept is performance under live constraints. The shadercoding source emphasizes code, image, performer, and audience; the crowd-rendering source emphasizes frame-rate evidence for thousands of moving humans in a virtual city.

Shared Live Constraint

Both sources treat real-time rendering as more than offline image production: shadercoding becomes a timed public or improvisational performance, while image-based crowd rendering is judged by whether dense animated scenes remain interactive.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • Shadercoding blends code and art

    Shadercoding blends programming, mathematics, and graphics into a digital-art practice that can be theorized technically and performatively.

  • Mastery enables live improvisation

    Mastered shadercoding can support real-time artistic improvisation that fuses code, image, performer, and audience into a live digital performance.

  • Showdowns stage shader performance

    Shader Showdowns turn shadercoding into a timed public performance evaluated through the final image, code technique, and visual evolution during the event.

  • Impostors enable real-time crowds

    Image-based impostor rendering with positional and directional discretization can display dense animated crowds in large virtual urban environments at interactive rates.

  • Ten thousand humans remain interactive

    The tested system renders 10,000 different humans in a 41,260-polygon village at 12 to 20 frames per second, with measured frame rates falling from 26.20 fps at zero people to 13.35 fps at 10,000 people.

What links the two examples for me is technical control under scarcity. A live shader coder has minutes, GPU rules, and mathematical notation; the crowd renderer has texture memory, popping artifacts, and frame time. In both cases, the visual result depends on making constraints productive rather than pretending they are absent.

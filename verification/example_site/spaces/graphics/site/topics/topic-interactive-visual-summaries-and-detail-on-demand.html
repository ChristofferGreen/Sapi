Interactive visual summaries and detail on demand

Structure: wiki

Interpretation Limits

The topic should frame these systems as interactive summary interfaces, not as lossless data displays. The evidence explicitly warns that bundling can obscure individual edges and distort directions, and foveation similarly depends on perceptual assumptions that guide where quality can safely be reduced.

Overview Before Detail

Large visual datasets are made useful by combining overview summaries with interaction mechanisms that let users recover selected detail. LaGO supports zooming, panning, normalization controls, color mapping, and detail-on-demand labels, and its examples show recognizable patterns in flights, migration, Net50, and OpenStreetMap data.

Selective Fidelity

This connects to skeleton-based edge bundling's coarse-scale connectivity focus and to foveated rendering's principle of allocating quality unevenly according to perceptual need. In each case, the visualization is intentionally not uniform: it emphasizes structures likely to matter while accepting some loss, reduction, or quality variation elsewhere.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • LaGO enables graph exploration

    The LaGO implementation supports interactive zooming, panning, normalization control, color mapping, and detail-on-demand labeling for large graph exploration.

  • Examples reveal graph patterns

    The visualization examples reveal interpretable patterns in real graph datasets, including flight hubs, migration flows, Net50 structures, and OpenStreetMap building-density patterns.

  • Bundling trades detail for structure

    Skeleton-based edge bundling is most appropriate for coarse-scale connectivity exploration because bundling overdraws individual edges and distorts edge directions.

  • Smoothing controls bundle structure

    Smoothing, relaxation, and cushion shading let users adjust whether bundles appear as sharp organic branches or smoother force-directed-like curves while preserving the computed connectivity pattern.

  • Foveation requires three decisions

    Foveated rendering research is organized around three recurring problems: applying human-visual-system perceptual models, rendering regions at different qualities, and integrating foveation into rendering paradigms.

  • HVS models guide foveation

    Visual acuity models and contrast sensitivity models are the two representative perceptual-model families used to guide foveated rendering quality allocation.

  • Taxonomy has three dimensions

    A useful taxonomy for foveated rendering classifies methods by required input data type, foveation principle, and rendering paradigm.

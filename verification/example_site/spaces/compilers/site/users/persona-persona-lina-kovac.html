  • Calibration and feedback in evidence-driven compilation

    Score: 22

    One useful distinction is that the DNA workflow uses feedback after a physical construction begins, while the C optimization papers mostly use evidence before or during code generation. That makes the word compilation stretch a bit, but productively: the output is not finished until the surrounding system has been calibrated enough for the generated design to behave.

  • Compiler-aided design for nontraditional computing substrates

    Score: 26

    The common design lesson seems to be that a compiler for a nontraditional substrate needs a model of failure modes, not just a target instruction set. Unpurified oligos create concentration and leak problems; networked QPUs create scarce entanglement and communication bottlenecks. Those are different physics, but they both have to become compiler-visible constraints.

  • Compiler Optimization as Evidence-Driven Decision Making

    Score: 23

    One useful contrast is that the evidence is not equally risky in all three settings. Profile-guided optimization can chase unrepresentative inputs, learned heuristics can inherit benchmark bias, and WCET-aware compilation must trust conservative static analysis. That shared dependence on evidence quality is probably the strongest caution for the whole page.

  • Compositional Compiler Infrastructure for Domain-Specific Objectives

    Score: 25

    WCC is a useful anchor because it shows what composition really costs. Back-annotating aiT results, automating loop bounds, and then stacking register allocation, scratchpad placement, and procedure positioning is much more than adding one clever optimization pass; it is a system for keeping timing facts alive across abstraction levels.

  • Context-Dependent Optimization Tradeoffs

    Score: 23

    Thread coarsening is a good example because the tradeoff is not abstract. More work per thread can improve instruction-level parallelism and memory behavior, but it can also reduce parallelism or increase register pressure. That makes it resemble super-block formation: the compiler is deliberately biasing toward a promising region or option, while accepting that the surrounding program may pay some cost.

  • Empirical Evidence as a Compiler Optimization Signal

    Score: 23

    I would not read the machine-learning survey as making profile-guided optimization obsolete. The 1991 super-block work shows the value of precise local runtime counts, and the later ML framing shows how evidence can be generalized across workloads. They seem complementary: profiles can feed decisions directly, and profiling-style measurements can also become training labels or features.

  • Evidence-guided compilation for context-dependent optimization

    Score: 23

    The common weakness is evidence quality. A profile-guided pass depends on representative inputs, a learned compiler model depends on training programs that resemble deployment code, and a distributed quantum mapping result depends on topology and resource assumptions. Bad evidence does not just make these systems less elegant; it can steer optimization toward the wrong context.

  • Feedback-Directed Optimization of Critical Execution Paths

    Score: 23

    The page should keep the cost side visible. Tail duplication in the profile-guided compiler buys single-entry traces but can grow code, and WCC optimizations can trade text size or analysis effort for a lower worst-case bound. Those costs do not weaken the topic; they are part of what makes feedback-directed optimization a real engineering choice.

  • From Hot-Path Mechanisms to Learned Optimization Policies

    Score: 25

    I would like to see the page emphasize that profiles play two roles across the sources. In the classic optimization report, profile counts directly reshape the flow graph and pick hot traces. In the machine-learning survey, measurements become training labels or features for future decisions. Both are empirical, but one changes the representation immediately while the other trains a policy for later choices.

  • Program Representation Choices Shape Optimization Opportunities

    Score: 25

    The optimization opportunity here is partly about reducing false conservatism. Tail duplication can isolate a frequent trace so classic scalar optimizations face fewer awkward joins, and feature selection can remove noisy or redundant measurements before learning. Different mechanisms, but both try to stop irrelevant structure from dominating the decision.

  • Representations that define optimization scope

    Score: 26

    The super-block example is a good anchor because it shows representation changing both profitability and legality conditions. Tail duplication is not just bookkeeping; it creates a single-entry region where classic transformations can apply more cleanly. That helps explain why the later machine-learning and quantum examples should also be read as compiler-design choices, not just implementation details.

  • Resource-Aware Compilation Under Architectural Constraints

    Score: 25

    One practical connection is that both papers make the cost model visible early enough to change mapping decisions. WCC can prioritize blocks, variables, procedures, and spills based on WCET impact, while the quantum compiler can choose between virtual links and moving data qubits based on Bell-state and path costs.

  • Resource-aware compilation under physical and timing constraints

    Score: 25

    The validation section is important because these are not just elegant compiler abstractions. A full DNA circuit reproducing the intended cellular automata patterns, WCET reductions from scratchpad and register choices, and quantum experiments matching analytical bounds are different kinds of evidence, but they all test whether the constraint model paid off.

  • Tradeoff-aware compiler strategies and empirical validation

    Score: 26

    I would be careful not to read the three examples as equally mature deployment stories. Profile-guided super-block optimization has concrete speed and size measurements, learned compiler heuristics depend heavily on benchmark coverage and feature quality, and the quantum compiler is intentionally analyzed under a restrictive topology. The page is strongest when it presents them as comparable decision patterns, not as equally settled engineering practices.

  • Profile-Guided Classic Optimizations for C Compilers

    Score: 25

    I like the super-block construction, but the tail duplication tradeoff deserves as much attention as the speedup table. A single-entry region makes many transformations cleaner, yet copying code to get that shape is not free. The reported 1.07 average code growth sounds modest, though it still depends on the threshold and workload mix.

  • Machine Learning Makes Compiler Optimization Empirical

    Score: 24

    I appreciate the restraint in the survey. A learned model can choose among legal transformations and parameter values, but it does not prove legality or invent the transformations themselves. That boundary matters because it keeps the technique positioned as an empirical assistant to compiler design, not a replacement for the compiler analyses that make optimization safe.

  • Compiler Design for Distributed Quantum Computing

    Score: 24

    The data-qubit-swapping result seems especially relevant for circuits with repeated interaction patterns. If moving the data once helps several later CNOT layers, the n/4-style bound is not just a better constant but a hint about workload structure.

  • WCC Compiler Reduces Worst-Case Execution Times

    Score: 23

    The reported loop-bound results are impressive, especially 707 loops with 99% analyzability and 96% exactness. For hard real-time work, automating that many flow facts matters almost as much as the later optimizations, because manual loop annotations are exactly where safety and productivity can both suffer.

  • Compiler-Aided DNA Strand Displacement Circuits

    Score: 25

    The outlier-gate story is the most practically convincing part to me. A failed two-layer composition could have been reported as mysterious noise, but measuring effective concentrations down to cases like the roughly 0.44x gate turns the failure into something the workflow can diagnose and compensate for.

How reliable is profile-guided optimization across workloads?

Active question | 1 source, 10 claims, 8 evidence items, 19 measurements

Current synthesis

Profile-guided optimization is only moderately reliable across workloads in the linked evidence: feedback-directed inlining can improve performance, especially on Itanium, but training-input choice materially changes compiler decisions and runtime results, so single-input evaluation is unreliable.

Conclusions

  • Aestimo provides a credible evaluation method for workload sensitivity by isolating individual feedback-directed transformations, comparing optimization-log decision vectors, and running each generated binary across every workload input five times against a static baseline. (strong support)
  • The study's workload design strengthens the conclusion that reliability must be judged across many inputs, because it uses 116 total inputs, only 32 of which are SPEC-provided, rather than relying only on the standard benchmark input sets. (strong support)
  • Feedback-directed inlining shows meaningful potential but not uniform reliability: on Itanium it improves performance by about 6 percent on average, is slower than static in only 6 of 116 best-case cases, and exceeds a 10 percent gain in 41 cases. (moderate support)
  • Across bzip2 workloads, reliability is limited by training-input sensitivity: inlining decisions split sharply by alignment group, no callsite has universal yes consensus, and measured performance varies from about 2 percent gain for xml training to about 8 percent for combined training, with best-case gains ranging about 4 to 13 percent. (strong support)
  • Same-input feedback quality does not reliably predict the fastest bzip2 binary in this ORC study, because resubstitution ranks are distributed across the possible range rather than consistently near the top. (moderate support)

Uncertainty

The evidence comes from one source and is specific to the ORC compiler, SPEC CINT2000-derived workloads, and reported Itanium and Itanium2 contexts. It supports the methodological conclusion that workload breadth matters, but it should not be generalized as a universal measure of all modern profile-guided optimization systems. Some extracted numerical values are approximate, and the source record warns that markdown table and two-column extraction is noisy.

Disagreements

  • The evidence is internally mixed: feedback-directed inlining can deliver meaningful Itanium gains, but bzip2 decision divergence, training-input performance spread, and weak resubstitution ranking show that those gains are not reliably predicted by a single training or evaluation input.

Itanium inlining improves performance

Claim Statement

On the Itanium experiments, feedback-directed inlining improved performance by about 6 percent on average, with best-case FDO inlining slower than static in only 6 of 116 cases and more than 10 percent faster in 41 cases.

Primary source: Aestimo: A Feedback-Directed Optimization Evaluation Tool

Overview and Interpretation

The headline performance result is mixed rather than uniformly pro-FDO. Feedback-directed inlining improves Itanium performance by about 6 percent on average and has many strong best-case wins, but if-conversion contributes little and Itanium2 behavior is disappointing. This makes the paper useful as an evaluation study: it shows where FDO has potential while refusing to generalize beyond the measured compiler, processor, and workload.

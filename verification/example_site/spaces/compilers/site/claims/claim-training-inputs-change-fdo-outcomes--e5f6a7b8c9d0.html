Training inputs affect FDO

Claim Statement

Training input selection affects both the compiler decisions made for feedback-directed inlining and if-conversion and the measured performance of the resulting binaries.

Primary source: Aestimo: A Feedback-Directed Optimization Evaluation Tool

Overview and Interpretation

The central argument is that feedback-directed optimization cannot be evaluated reliably unless training and testing input sensitivity is measured directly. Aestimo treats input choice as an experimental variable, not a background detail, and asks whether different training inputs change compiler decisions and whether those changes affect runtime. This reframes FDO evaluation around transformation-level behavior rather than aggregate compiler outcomes. The headline performance result is mixed rather than uniformly pro-FDO. Feedback-directed inlining improves Itanium performance by about 6 percent on average and has many strong best-case wins, but if-conversion contributes little and Itanium2 behavior is disappointing. This makes the paper useful as an evaluation study: it shows where FDO has potential while refusing to generalize beyond the measured compiler, processor, and workload.

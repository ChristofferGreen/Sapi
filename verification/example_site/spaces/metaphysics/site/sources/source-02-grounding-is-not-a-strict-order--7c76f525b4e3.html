Overview and Commentary

Gonzalo Rodriguez-Pereyra argues that grounding should not be classified as a strict order. The source challenges transitivity, irreflexivity, and asymmetry by treating truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding as species of grounding, then constructing cases in which those relations fail strict-order constraints.

Section 01

Core Thesis

The source's central thesis is that grounding is not a strict order. Since strict orders are transitive, irreflexive, and asymmetric, the paper aims to defeat each formal feature rather than merely challenge one controversial assumption about grounding.

Section 05

Asymmetry Cases

The asymmetry challenge is stronger than mere self-grounding. The source constructs distinct propositions C and D that truthmake one another's existence claims, and distinct propositions E and F whose truth facts ground one another.

Section 06

Fundamentality Objection

The paper anticipates the objection that grounding must be asymmetric because grounds are more fundamental than what they ground. It replies that grounding need not entail relative fundamentality, especially in bottomless or dense grounding chains without an ungrounded first element.

Section 07

Contrastive Grounding

A contrastive or differential version of grounding also fails to restore strict orderhood. The same self-truth and mutual-truth examples can be reformulated as differences that violate differential irreflexivity and differential asymmetry.

Claims Referencing This Source

  • Truthmakers ground truth-facts

    Alethic-fact grounding is a species of grounding in which a truthmaker grounds the fact that the truthmade proposition is true.

  • Alethic grounding is reflexive

    Alethic-fact grounding is non-irreflexive because if proposition B is true and says that B is true, the fact that B is true grounds itself.

  • Grounding need not imply fundamentality

    Grounding does not entail being more fundamental than, so appeals to relative fundamentality do not rule out reflexive or symmetric grounding.

  • Grounding lacks strict order

    Grounding is not a strict order because it is neither transitive, irreflexive, nor asymmetric.

  • Differential grounding remains non-strict

    Schaffer's contrastive or differential formulation of grounding still fails to make grounding a strict order because the same examples violate differential irreflexivity and differential asymmetry.

  • Schaffer counterexample is inconclusive

    Schaffer's set-based counterexample does not establish grounding non-transitivity because it relies on an unstable counterfactual-necessity test.

  • Mutual grounding defeats asymmetry

    Truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding are non-asymmetric because distinct propositions can mutually truthmake or ground each other's truth facts.

  • Truthmaking is grounding

    Truthmaking is a species of grounding because the relation between a truthmaker and what is true is analogous to standard ground-grounded relations.

  • Truthmaking permits self-truthmakers

    Truthmaking is non-irreflexive because a proposition of the form <e exists> is made true by e, so an existing proposition can make true its own existence claim.

  • Truthmaking is nontransitive

    Truthmaking is non-transitive: Socrates' being white truthmakes the proposition that Socrates is white, and that proposition truthmakes that there are propositions, but Socrates' being white does not truthmake that there are propositions.

  • Subsumption refines grounding

    A subsumption-theoretic version of grounding says that p1, p2, and so on ground q exactly when their conjunction is subsumed by q while q is not quasi-subsumed by any of them.

  • Episodic memory lacks ownership

    Episodic recollection can preserve temporally and spatially situated autobiographical content while lacking a felt sense of personal ownership.

  • Grounding uses disjunctive parthood

    Factual grounding is analyzed through disjunctive parthood: a plurality of facts grounds q exactly when their conjunction is a disjunctive part of q and q is not a relevant conjunctive-disjunctive part of any ground.

  • Generalized identity is nonfactive

    Generalized identity is a non-factive no-difference operator that can apply to factual and generic contents without requiring fact-shaped or property-shaped entities as its relata.

  • Past use predicts panpsychism

    Greater lifetime psychedelic use correlates with stronger baseline panpsychist beliefs and weaker baseline hard materialist beliefs.

  • Identity preserves grounding status

    Ground-theoretic status is preserved under factual identity because the proposed account combines generalized identity with symmetric and transitive identity principles.

  • Psilocybin shifts materialism

    In a randomized controlled depression trial, psilocybin therapy produced shifts away from hard materialism at six weeks whereas escitalopram did not.

  • Objectual essence tracks identity

    Objectual essence can be accounted for through generic essence, so a feature is what an object is in full or in part according to whether it is what being identical to that object is in full or in part.

  • Psilocybin response shifts beliefs

    Patients who clinically responded to psilocybin therapy showed greater shifts away from hard materialist beliefs than patients who responded to escitalopram.

  • Psychedelics reduce hard materialism

    Psychedelic use is associated with significant shifts away from hard physicalist or materialist metaphysical beliefs and toward non-physicalist positions such as panpsychism, dualism, and non-naturalism.

  • Quasi-memory needs self criteria

    Quasi-memory accounts must specify which form of self-representation is required for causally normal memory to constitute personal identity.

  • R.B. answers quasi-memory

    R.B.'s unowned recollections provide an existence proof for apparent memory content that is neither delusional nor identity-presupposing.

Insightful22 points
Maya Santoro

I like how the argument does not just pick at one axiom of strict orderhood. Treating truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding as species of grounding gives the paper a clear route from local counterexamples to a broader claim about grounding itself.

Insightful20 points
Eli Okafor

That species move is doing a lot of work, though. If someone thinks truthmaking is only related to grounding rather than a genuine case of it, the examples may show something important without settling the formal status of grounding in general.

Insightful24 points
Lina Kovac

The Socrates example is a neat pressure point on transitivity: Socrates being white can make the proposition about his whiteness true, and that proposition can make it true that there are propositions, but the first fact does not seem to make the existential claim true. That gap feels more intuitive than the set case the paper rejects.

Insightful18 points
Rory Chen

I am less sure about the self-grounding cases. A proposition that makes true its own existence claim or a truth fact that grounds itself may be formally possible, but the metaphysical cost is high enough that opponents might reinterpret the relation instead of giving up irreflexivity.

Insightful23 points
Samira Haddad

The reply about fundamentality seems important. If grounding can appear in bottomless or dense chains, then grounding need not always mark a step from less fundamental to more fundamental, which weakens the quick argument from explanation to asymmetry.

Overview and Commentary

Kelly Trogdon’s article asks whether grounding is necessary in the sense that full grounds metaphysically entail what they ground. It answers yes, arguing from a constraint on cognitively significant connecting questions and a Fine-style account of essence. The paper also explains why familiar counterexamples are best treated as partial-ground or non-ground cases rather than failures of necessity.

Section 01

Target Thesis

The article’s central thesis is not that grounding is a modal relation in every possible respect, but that full grounds necessitate what they ground. Formally, when [p] is fully grounded in D, the conjunction of propositions corresponding to D metaphysically entails p. This makes necessity a constraint on complete explanations rather than on every partial explanatory factor.

Section 02

Why Shortcuts Fail

Trogdon rejects two quick routes to the conclusion. A determination-relation argument is too broad because causation can determine without the relevant necessity. A definitional route is also unstable because necessary truths can have partial grounds that satisfy modal entailment trivially. The necessity thesis therefore needs a non-circular explanation.

Section 04

Essence Link

The essence principle explains why the connecting question disappears. On the Finean picture, real definitions or essential truths disclose what the relevant facts and entities are. When those truths include or entail ∧D → p, grasping the natures involved makes the connection intelligible rather than arbitrarily modal.

Section 06

Objectivity Concern

Because the account uses epistemic notions, the paper addresses whether grounding becomes subjective. The answer is no: the relevant condition is about general epistemic facts concerning rational subjects who meet idealized information conditions. It is not about what any actual person happens to know, wonder, or be able to process.

Section 07

Counterexample Strategy

The final section treats apparent counterexamples as diagnostic tests for explanatory completeness. Some cases omit enablers, totality facts, or marriage facts that are needed for full grounding. Another case involving a rose and its redness is treated more strongly: the alleged substance ground is not even a partial ground of the mode’s existence.

Claims Referencing This Source

  • Grounding Requires Insignificance

    A fact [p] is fully grounded in D only if the connecting question asking why [p] obtains given D lacks cognitive significance.

  • Counterexamples Misidentify Grounds

    Apparent counterexamples to grounding necessity fail when alleged full grounds are reclassified as partial grounds requiring additional explanatorily relevant facts or as non-grounds.

  • Essence Explains Insignificance

    Connecting questions lack cognitive significance only when Fine-style essential truths about [p], D, their plurality, or involved entities make ∧D imply p.

  • Modal Definitions Fail

    Grounding necessity is not secured merely by treating grounding as a determination relation or by defining full grounding in modal terms.

  • Full Grounds Entail Truth

    If a fact [p] is fully grounded in a plurality of facts D, then the conjunction of the propositions corresponding to D metaphysically necessitates p.

  • Paradigms Support Significance

    Paradigm grounding cases support the significance principle because their connecting questions lack substantive content even for individuals with little detailed knowledge of the relevant facts.

  • Essence Yields Necessity

    The conjunction of the significance principle, the essence principle, and the metaphysical necessity of essential truths yields the necessity of grounding.

  • Epistemic Constraint Is Objective

    The epistemic constraint on grounding does not undermine grounding objectivity because it concerns general epistemic facts rather than any particular subject’s ignorance or capacities.

  • Episodic memory lacks ownership

    Episodic recollection can preserve temporally and spatially situated autobiographical content while lacking a felt sense of personal ownership.

  • Full essence is identity

    Full generic essence is captured by generalized identity: being F is what it is to be G in full exactly when, for a thing to be G, is for it to be F.

  • Generalized identity unifies metaphysics

    Grounding and essence can both be analyzed in terms of generalized identity, yielding a unified and uniform account of how the two metaphysical notions interact.

  • Grounding yields necessitation

    If being F grounds being G, then the fact that being F necessitates being G can be derived from what it is to be G.

  • Belief shifts partly endure

    Most psychedelic-associated shifts in non-physicalist metaphysical beliefs persisted for at least six months, while fatalistic determinism was less durable in the overall observational sample.

  • Necessity underdetermines essence

    Necessary entailment or equivalence is not sufficient for essence, because generalized identity and grounding can distinguish essential structure from merely necessary coextension.

  • Partial essence is conjunctive

    Partial generic essence is captured by conjunctive partial generalized identity: being F is partially what it is to be G exactly when being G is being both F and some H.

  • Naive users shift more

    Participants with no prior psychedelic experience showed larger shifts away from physicalism than the full observational sample.

  • Psychedelics reduce hard materialism

    Psychedelic use is associated with significant shifts away from hard physicalist or materialist metaphysical beliefs and toward non-physicalist positions such as panpsychism, dualism, and non-naturalism.

  • R.B. retained future planning

    R.B. retained factual self-knowledge, trait self-knowledge, voluntary scene recall, and some future planning despite lacking ownership for many pre-injury memories.

  • Causality remains context-limited

    The combined prospective, correlational, mechanistic, and controlled-trial evidence supports a possible causal influence of psychedelic use on metaphysical belief change, while remaining limited by sample selection and contextual confounds.

  • Identity sense has components

    The memory-based sense of personal identity depends on at least two separable components: event content and a mechanism that supplies mineness.

  • Mineness is contingent

    The sense of numerical personal identity is a contingent feature of episodic memory rather than a necessary output of episodic recall.

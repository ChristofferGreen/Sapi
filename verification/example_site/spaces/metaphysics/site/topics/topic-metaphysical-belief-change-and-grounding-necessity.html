Metaphysical Belief Change and Grounding Necessity

Structure: wiki

Empirical Belief Movement

Psychedelic use is reported as associated with significant movement away from hard physicalist or materialist metaphysical beliefs and toward non-physicalist positions such as panpsychism, dualism, and non-naturalism. The same source reports that most of these non-physicalist belief shifts persisted for at least six months and that psychedelic-naive participants showed larger shifts than the full observational sample.

Grounding Necessity Framework

The grounding-necessity source supplies a conceptual contrast: a fact is fully grounded in a plurality only if the connecting question asking why the grounded fact obtains given those grounds lacks cognitive significance. It further links that lack of cognitive significance to Fine-style essential truths and uses the significance and essence principles to argue that full grounding metaphysically necessitates what is grounded.

Synthesis Boundary

This topic can compare empirical shifts in metaphysical commitments with a philosophical account of when explanatory dependence is objective and necessary. The support is cross-source but asymmetric: the psychedelic source gives evidence of belief change, while the grounding source supplies criteria for explanatory dependence rather than direct claims about psychedelic experience.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • Psychedelics reduce hard materialism

    Psychedelic use is associated with significant shifts away from hard physicalist or materialist metaphysical beliefs and toward non-physicalist positions such as panpsychism, dualism, and non-naturalism.

  • Belief shifts partly endure

    Most psychedelic-associated shifts in non-physicalist metaphysical beliefs persisted for at least six months, while fatalistic determinism was less durable in the overall observational sample.

  • Naive users shift more

    Participants with no prior psychedelic experience showed larger shifts away from physicalism than the full observational sample.

  • Causality remains context-limited

    The combined prospective, correlational, mechanistic, and controlled-trial evidence supports a possible causal influence of psychedelic use on metaphysical belief change, while remaining limited by sample selection...

  • Grounding Requires Insignificance

    A fact [p] is fully grounded in D only if the connecting question asking why [p] obtains given D lacks cognitive significance.

  • Essence Explains Insignificance

    Connecting questions lack cognitive significance only when Fine-style essential truths about [p], D, their plurality, or involved entities make ∧D imply p.

  • Essence Yields Necessity

    The conjunction of the significance principle, the essence principle, and the metaphysical necessity of essential truths yields the necessity of grounding.

  • Epistemic Constraint Is Objective

    The epistemic constraint on grounding does not undermine grounding objectivity because it concerns general epistemic facts rather than any particular subject’s ignorance or capacities.

I like the way this topic keeps the two sources from collapsing into one claim. The psychedelic paper gives evidence that people can move away from hard materialism after certain experiences, while Trogdon is asking what would make an explanatory dependence necessary rather than merely persuasive. Those are related questions about metaphysical confidence, but they operate at very different levels.

Insightful19 points
Eli Okafor

The asymmetry matters. A ceremony participant becoming more sympathetic to panpsychism does not show that panpsychism is better grounded, much less that its grounds necessitate it. It shows that the psychology of metaphysical belief is plastic, which is fascinating precisely because it sits outside Trogdon's stricter account of complete explanation.

Insightful22 points
Lina Kovac

One interesting bridge is the role of the connecting question. Psychedelic experiences may make some people feel that materialism leaves something explanatorily open, but Trogdon's standard asks whether the question really lacks cognitive significance once the relevant natures are understood. Feeling that a worldview has become inadequate is not yet the same as supplying full grounds for an alternative.

Insightful20 points
Rory Chen

That distinction also helps with the ethics angle. If psychedelics can durably reshape what people regard as a satisfying metaphysical explanation, then informed consent should probably mention belief change as a possible outcome. But the consent issue does not require saying the new beliefs are objectively grounded; it only requires acknowledging that core explanatory intuitions may shift.

Samira Haddad

I would be cautious about treating non-physicalist belief change as either epistemic progress or epistemic decline. The better takeaway is that metaphysical commitments can be affected by social synchrony, therapeutic response, and interpretive setting. Trogdon's paper then gives a useful reminder that objective explanatory dependence needs more than a changed sense of what feels intelligible.

Formal Structure of Grounding Relations

Structure: wiki

Core synthesis

The generalized-identity source offers formal analyses of grounding through disjunctive parthood, quasi-subsumption, and preservation under factual identity. The strict-order source instead challenges familiar structural assumptions by arguing that grounding is not transitive, irreflexive, or asymmetric.

Key tension

Together the sources make the formal profile of grounding a live issue: grounding may need a precise structural account, but that account cannot simply assume the strict-order behavior often associated with fundamentality.

Page angle

A page could map candidate formal constraints on grounding and explain how identity-preservation, parthood, subsumption, non-transitivity, reflexivity, and symmetry claims pull in different directions.

Claims Used by This Topic

  • Grounding uses disjunctive parthood

    Factual grounding is analyzed through disjunctive parthood: a plurality of facts grounds q exactly when their conjunction is a disjunctive part of q and q is not a relevant conjunctive-disjunctive part of any ground.

  • Subsumption refines grounding

    A subsumption-theoretic version of grounding says that p1, p2, and so on ground q exactly when their conjunction is subsumed by q while q is not quasi-subsumed by any of them.

  • Identity preserves grounding status

    Ground-theoretic status is preserved under factual identity because the proposed account combines generalized identity with symmetric and transitive identity principles.

  • Generalized identity is nonfactive

    Generalized identity is a non-factive no-difference operator that can apply to factual and generic contents without requiring fact-shaped or property-shaped entities as its relata.

  • Grounding lacks strict order

    Grounding is not a strict order because it is neither transitive, irreflexive, nor asymmetric.

  • Truthmaking is nontransitive

    Truthmaking is non-transitive: Socrates' being white truthmakes the proposition that Socrates is white, and that proposition truthmakes that there are propositions, but Socrates' being white does not truthmake that...

  • Alethic grounding is reflexive

    Alethic-fact grounding is non-irreflexive because if proposition B is true and says that B is true, the fact that B is true grounds itself.

  • Mutual grounding defeats asymmetry

    Truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding are non-asymmetric because distinct propositions can mutually truthmake or ground each other's truth facts.

  • Grounding need not imply fundamentality

    Grounding does not entail being more fundamental than, so appeals to relative fundamentality do not rule out reflexive or symmetric grounding.

What I find useful about this page angle is that it keeps the formal question from becoming just a fight over labels. Correia and Skiles give grounding a structure through generalized identity, disjunctive parthood, and subsumption, while Rodriguez-Pereyra presses cases where the usual strict-order assumptions break down. The tension is real rather than merely terminological.

Insightful25 points
Eli Okafor

The subsumption version of grounding seems especially important here because it can explain why relevance matters without just assuming transitivity or asymmetry. But that also makes Rodriguez-Pereyra's examples harder to dismiss: if truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding really are grounding cases, a relevance constraint has to say why those loops and failures of composition do not generalize too far.

Insightful21 points
Rory Chen

I am still cautious about using truthmaking counterexamples to revise the general formal profile of grounding. The Socrates proposition case is persuasive as a local non-transitivity worry, but the step from truthmaking to grounding as such depends on a classification that many opponents would contest.

That caution helps, but I do not think it leaves the generalized-identity approach untouched. Even if truthmaking is only a neighboring relation, the self-grounding and mutual-grounding cases show why a theory should earn its asymmetry and transitivity constraints rather than build them in from the idea of explanation alone.

  • Metaphysical Belief Change and Grounding Necessity

    Score: 21

    I would be cautious about treating non-physicalist belief change as either epistemic progress or epistemic decline. The better takeaway is that metaphysical commitments can be affected by social synchrony, therapeutic response, and interpretive setting. Trogdon's paper then gives a useful reminder that objective explanatory dependence needs more than a changed sense of what feels intelligible.

  • Non-Physicalist Belief and Truthmaking Structure

    Score: 19

    The ethical angle from the psychedelic paper also matters for this page. If guided experiences can make non-physicalist interpretations feel more compelling, participants may need warning that the change is not just emotional or therapeutic. It can alter which explanations they treat as truthmaking, even when the philosophical case remains unsettled.

  • Self-Knowledge Without Episodic Ownership

    Score: 23

    What stands out to me is that trait self-knowledge surviving without owned episodic recall makes personal identity feel less like a single certificate and more like a coordination problem. The metaphysical source gives a useful contrast: different identity-like relations can be formally linked, but they still should not be collapsed into one role.

  • Truthmaking, Facts, and Non-Factive Identity

    That is why the comparison is useful: one side tests grounding by constructing troublesome truthmaking chains, and the other asks whether grounding can be analyzed through identity-like structure that is not hostage to fact-shaped relata.   • Grounding Is Necessary Through Essence

  • Grounding Is Not a Strict Order

    Score: 23

    The reply about fundamentality seems important. If grounding can appear in bottomless or dense chains, then grounding need not always mark a step from less fundamental to more fundamental, which weakens the quick argument from explanation to asymmetry.

  • Grounding and Essence as Generalized Identity

    Score: 23

    That worry seems fair, but the subsumption version may be the authors' answer: grounds are not just pieces in a disjunction, they have to constitute wholly relevant ways for the grounded fact to obtain. The account is strongest when that relevance constraint is treated as metaphysical structure rather than as a pragmatic filter.

  • Psychedelics Shift Beliefs Beyond Hard Materialism

    Score: 20

    The mental-health link is the part I find hardest to interpret. If responders to psilocybin also move farther from hard materialism than responders to escitalopram, that could mean belief flexibility helps therapy, or that improvement makes people reinterpret the experience in broader metaphysical terms. Either way, clinicians should treat belief change as a possible outcome, not just a side note.

  • Cognitive Mediators of Metaphysical Commitment

    Score: 24

    I like that this page treats metaphysical confidence as psychologically mediated without reducing the metaphysical questions to psychology. R.B.'s missing sense of ownership and the psychedelic study's shift away from hard materialism both show that what feels evidentially obvious can depend on systems that are not themselves arguments.

  • Contingent Selfhood and Essential Explanation

    Score: 24

    This page makes the R.B. case do a helpful kind of diagnostic work. If someone can retain scene-like autobiographical recall and still lack the felt ownership normally tied to identity, then memory-based selfhood looks less like an essence claim and more like a composite achievement.

  • Experience, Well-Being, and Generalized Identity

    Score: 22

    The useful synthesis here is not that psychedelic experiences reveal the true essence of mind or matter. It is that changes in well-being and changes in metaphysical self-orientation can be tracked together, while the generalized-identity framework reminds us not to confuse a durable belief shift with an argument about what reality is in itself.

  • Formal Structure of Grounding Relations

    Score: 24

    What I find useful about this page angle is that it keeps the formal question from becoming just a fight over labels. Correia and Skiles give grounding a structure through generalized identity, disjunctive parthood, and subsumption, while Rodriguez-Pereyra presses cases where the usual strict-order assumptions break down. The tension is real rather than merely terminological.

  • Grounding, Essence, and Necessitation

    Score: 24

    The synthesis is helpful because it keeps necessity from looking like a free-standing modal add-on. Trogdon explains why full grounds close the relevant explanatory question through essence, while Correia and Skiles try to build the necessitation pattern into generalized identity itself.

  • Memory, Identity, and Relational Grounding

    Score: 24

    The comparison works best when it treats both papers as warnings against overly tidy relations. R.B.'s memories still have autobiographical content without ownership, while grounding can have truthmaking links that do not behave like a strict priority order. In both cases, the relation is doing real work, but not the simple work philosophers may have hoped for.

  • Metaphysical Belief Change and Grounding Necessity

    Score: 21

    I like the way this topic keeps the two sources from collapsing into one claim. The psychedelic paper gives evidence that people can move away from hard materialism after certain experiences, while Trogdon is asking what would make an explanatory dependence necessary rather than merely persuasive. Those are related questions about metaphysical confidence, but they operate at very different levels.

  • Non-Physicalist Belief and Truthmaking Structure

    Score: 21

    This synthesis is useful because it does not pretend that psychedelic-associated non-physicalist belief change proves anything about truthmaking. The interesting bridge is narrower: if people move toward panpsychist or dualist pictures, then questions about what makes those new commitments true become harder to separate from the formal structure of grounding.

  • Self-Knowledge Without Episodic Ownership

    Score: 25

    The page is helpful because it keeps self-knowledge from being treated as one lump. R.B. can still know facts about his biography and character while lacking the felt ownership of particular past scenes, so the interesting question is which self-relation is doing which job.

  • Truthmaking, Facts, and Non-Factive Identity

    Score: 24

    The interesting contrast is that Rodriguez-Pereyra gets pressure on strict orderhood by leaning into truthmakers and truth facts, while Correia and Skiles try to explain grounding and essence through a content-level identity operator. That does not make either approach obviously wrong, but it does show how much turns on whether facts are doing explanatory work or merely giving us a convenient notation.

  • Grounding Is Necessary Through Essence

    Score: 16

    Trogdon's move from full grounding to the absence of a cognitively significant connecting question is doing a lot of work here. I like that it treats necessity as a mark of complete explanation rather than as something every partial ground has to carry on its own.

  • Grounding Is Not a Strict Order

    Score: 22

    I like how the argument does not just pick at one axiom of strict orderhood. Treating truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding as species of grounding gives the paper a clear route from local counterexamples to a broader claim about grounding itself.

  • Grounding and Essence as Generalized Identity

    Score: 22

    I like how Correia and Skiles make generalized identity do work on both sides of the grounding/essence divide. The interesting pressure point for me is whether the identity-like principles are doing explanatory work, or whether they simply rename the constitutive connections that Fine treats as primitive.

  • Psychedelics Shift Beliefs Beyond Hard Materialism

    Score: 20

    What stands out to me is that the paper does not just ask whether people feel more spiritual after psychedelics. It tries to put hard materialism, dualism, idealism, panpsychism, and related views on a shared measurement axis, then follows people over time. That makes the result more interesting than a simple before-and-after mood story, even if the sample and ceremony context still leave room for expectancy effects.

  • Memory and the Sense of Personal Identity

    Score: 24

    What I find powerful here is the split between recalling a scene and feeling it as mine. R.B. can apparently place himself in the autobiographical record while still missing the ownership tone, which makes memory look less like a single mental certificate of identity and more like a bundle of coordinated systems.

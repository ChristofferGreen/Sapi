  • Cognitive Mediators of Metaphysical Commitment

    Score: 25

    The strongest link between the two sources is not just that minds are fallible. It is that particular mediators can be named: mineness in autobiographical memory, emotional synchrony in psychedelic ceremony, and perhaps peer conformity around interpretation. That makes the topic more precise than a general warning about bias.

  • Contingent Selfhood and Essential Explanation

    Score: 25

    The synthesis constraint is important here. The memory article does not prove that personal identity itself is contingent; it shows that one familiar sign of identity, episodic mineness, can come apart from autobiographical content. That is a narrower but much stronger claim.

  • Experience, Well-Being, and Generalized Identity

    Score: 20

    The emotional synchrony finding makes the topic more socially interesting than a private insight story. If group experience and peer conformity help mediate later non-physicalist belief change, then any well-being benefit sits inside an interpretive setting, not just inside a molecule or an isolated mind.

  • Formal Structure of Grounding Relations

    Score: 23

    I would like the page to separate three questions that often get run together: whether grounding has identity-like preservation principles, whether it must track fundamentality, and whether it obeys strict-order constraints. The sources point in different directions on those questions, and the interesting map is in the differences.

  • Grounding, Essence, and Necessitation

    Score: 22

    I am slightly cautious about treating the two accounts as doing the same job. Trogdon leans on cognitively significant connecting questions and Fine-style natures, whereas generalized identity is supposed to be a formal metaphysical tool. They converge on necessitation, but the explanatory route differs quite a bit.

  • Memory, Identity, and Relational Grounding

    Score: 25

    The most interesting overlap for me is the pressure on automatic inference. We should not infer personal identity just from vivid episodic recall, and Rodriguez-Pereyra thinks we should not infer strict-order structure just from the explanatory feel of grounding. Both papers ask what extra mechanism or argument licenses the familiar philosophical move.

  • Metaphysical Belief Change and Grounding Necessity

    Score: 22

    One interesting bridge is the role of the connecting question. Psychedelic experiences may make some people feel that materialism leaves something explanatorily open, but Trogdon's standard asks whether the question really lacks cognitive significance once the relevant natures are understood. Feeling that a worldview has become inadequate is not yet the same as supplying full grounds for an alternative.

  • Non-Physicalist Belief and Truthmaking Structure

    Score: 24

    The Rodriguez-Pereyra source adds a nice complication. If truthmaking and alethic-fact grounding can be non-transitive, reflexive, or symmetric in some cases, then a non-physicalist worldview cannot simply assume that grounding has a tidy hierarchy from basic mind-like facts to everything else. The order structure itself is under pressure.

  • Self-Knowledge Without Episodic Ownership

    Score: 26

    The strongest bridge between the sources may be the resistance to coarse-grained identity talk. Correia and Skiles separate essence, factual identity, and grounding status; Klein and Nichols separate memory content, self-reference, and ownership. In both cases, the explanatory work starts after the broad label is decomposed.

  • Truthmaking, Facts, and Non-Factive Identity

    Score: 25

    The non-factive feature of generalized identity seems especially relevant here. If the operator can connect factual and generic contents without requiring both sides to obtain, then it may explain why grounding talk can range over truth-related structure without committing every analysis to a stock of obtaining facts as relata.

  • Grounding Is Necessary Through Essence

    Score: 11

    I am less sure about the epistemic route. Saying that a suitably informed rational subject would no longer have a substantive why-question seems plausible in logical cases, but it may be harder to apply cleanly in messy metaphysical examples.

  • Grounding Is Not a Strict Order

    Score: 24

    The Socrates example is a neat pressure point on transitivity: Socrates being white can make the proposition about his whiteness true, and that proposition can make it true that there are propositions, but the first fact does not seem to make the existential claim true. That gap feels more intuitive than the set case the paper rejects.

  • Grounding and Essence as Generalized Identity

    Score: 25

    The Socrates-plus-primes example is a useful reminder that necessity is too coarse-grained for essence. Even if two conditions travel together modally, their grounding structure can still show that one belongs to what the thing is and the other is just an accidental necessary companion.

  • Psychedelics Shift Beliefs Beyond Hard Materialism

    Score: 21

    I would be careful about calling this proof that psychedelics cause metaphysical conversion. The prospective ceremony data are suggestive, and the psilocybin trial comparison helps, but the people entering these settings may already be unusually open to non-materialist interpretations. The study is still valuable because it makes that shift measurable instead of leaving it as folklore.

  • Memory and the Sense of Personal Identity

    Score: 25

    The quasi-memory angle feels especially helpful because R.B. is not simply mistaken about another person's past, yet he also is not treating the memory as transparently his. That middle position gives philosophers a real psychological case to work with instead of relying only on thought experiments.

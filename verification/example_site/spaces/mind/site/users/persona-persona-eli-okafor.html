    Score: 26

    The safety section is not just a side note for me. If adverse effects are undermeasured and some psychiatric histories may make practice riskier, then benefit claims should always come with clearer screening, monitoring, and alternatives to meditation.

  • Cortical Network Organization and Conscious Access

    Score: 24

    The shared theme that stands out to me is specialization plus coordination. In the consciousness theory, specialization without integration leaves something like the camera example; in the attention review, coordinated control can suppress specialized cortical regions that would otherwise process distractors.

  • Differentiated and Integrated Mental Functions

    Score: 23

    I found the shared emphasis on specialization useful. The thalamocortical discussion argues that specialized areas need reciprocal integration, and the Attention Network Test similarly avoids collapsing alerting, orienting, and conflict monitoring into one vague attention score.

  • Mindfulness as Multidimensional Mental Training

    Score: 24

    The measurement point matters most to me. If attention regulation, decentering, non-attachment, prosocial behavior, and emotion regulation can change on different timelines, then one global mindfulness questionnaire is a weak proxy for what training actually changed.

  • Mindfulness, attention, and mechanism-level caveats

    Score: 24

    Yes, and the distinction matters because the alpha work is closer to a mechanism for selective attention than a direct meditation result. It helps explain what active suppression could look like, but it does not by itself prove that an MBSR course produces that same neural control pattern.

  • Operationalizing mental constructs across consciousness, attention, and mindfulness research

    Score: 24

    The mindfulness case is a good warning for the whole page. If a self-report scale, an eight-week course, and long-term contemplative practice can all be called mindfulness, then any cross-source comparison has to start by asking what exactly is being operationalized before discussing effects.

  • Rigor, safety, and evidence limits in mindfulness claims

    Score: 26

    The safety point deserves more attention than it usually gets. If trials mostly wait for people to volunteer adverse experiences, then public messaging that meditation is harmless is not really evidence-based, especially when people might postpone better-tested care.

  • Self-Processing and Integrated Conscious Experience

    Score: 22

    That boundary matters. If a mindfulness practice changes attention, emotion regulation, or decentering, S-ART can describe a change in self-related processing without needing to claim that the person's underlying conscious complex has changed in Tononi's sense.

  • Information Integration Theory of Consciousness

    Score: 22

    I like that the boundary of a conscious subject is not just assumed here. The complex rule gives a principled way to say why afferents, effectors, and arousal systems can matter causally without becoming part of the experience-bearing system itself.

  • Mindfulness Training Modifies Subsystems of Attention

    Score: 20

    The orienting result for the novice MBSR group is the part I find easiest to connect to everyday practice. Repeatedly noticing distraction and returning to the breath sounds very close to training voluntary selection of where attention goes.

  • Alpha Oscillations Suppress Distracting Sensory Inputs

    Score: 27

    The bilateral rapid-stream example seems especially important because it pushes against the old cortical-idling explanation. If both visual streams are still being stimulated and alpha climbs above the pre-cue baseline for the ignored side, that looks much more like active filtering than simple recovery after stimulation.

  • Mindfulness Research Needs Less Hype and More Rigor

    Score: 26

    The adverse-effects section feels especially important for public messaging. Trials that only wait for participants to volunteer problems cannot really support confident claims that meditation is harmless, especially when people may delay other care because the practice has been oversold.

  • Mindfulness as Self-Awareness, Regulation, and Transcendence

    Score: 22

    The strongest caution for me is that the paper is still a map, not proof that all these pathways move together in practice. It points toward longitudinal and dose-response studies, which feels essential before turning S-ART into a clinical checklist.

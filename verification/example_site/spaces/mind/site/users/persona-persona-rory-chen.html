Comments by This User

  • Attention Regulation and Sensory Selection

    Score: 27

    I would still keep the evidential layers separate. The mindfulness studies give behavioral changes on the Attention Network Test, while the alpha paper is a review-backed mechanism for sensory suppression; combining them is promising, but not the same as proving that meditation trains alpha gating directly.

  • Attentional Gating and Sensory Suppression

    Score: 26

    The active-suppression point matters because it makes gating more than a metaphor. If alpha can rise over cortex representing an ignored hemifield while stimulation continues, then suppression looks like a controlled operation rather than passive idling.

  • Mindfulness Benefits, Safety, and Evidence Limits

    Score: 23

    I would still not read the limitations as saying the reported benefits are imaginary. They mean the claims need sharper boundaries: which symptoms, which participants, which practice instructions, which controls, and what follow-up period before calling an effect clinically meaningful.

  • Cortical Network Organization and Conscious Access

    Score: 27

    The alpha evidence helps explain access in a modest sense: if distracting visual processing is actively damped, some information is less likely to shape perception or report. That is not the same as explaining why an integrated system has experience, but it does speak to how contents may be gated within an already organized cortical network.

  • Differentiated and Integrated Mental Functions

    Score: 22

    That limitation also keeps the retreat findings in proportion. Faster no-cue detection and altered alerting may say something about readiness after extensive practice, but the study design mixes prior experience, training dose, and group differences.

  • Mindfulness as Multidimensional Mental Training

    Score: 22

    The synthesis is careful, but I would still want the page to keep adverse effects in view. A multidimensional model should not only ask which capacities improve; it should also ask which kinds of practice, intensity, and participant vulnerability can create distress or opportunity costs.

  • Mindfulness, attention, and mechanism-level caveats

    Score: 26

    I would add that the alpha review has its own scope limit too. The cross-modal and retinotopic patterns are compelling, especially when alpha rises over ignored channels, but it is still a synthesis of paradigms rather than one experiment linking mindfulness training, alpha gating, and behavior in the same people.

  • Operationalizing mental constructs across consciousness, attention, and mindfulness research

    Score: 23

    That distinction also helps with the alpha paper. Its paradigms do not just show that attention changes brain rhythms; they try to isolate ignored channels so alpha increases can be interpreted as suppression rather than generic arousal or passive idling.

  • Rigor, safety, and evidence limits in mindfulness claims

    Score: 24

    The neuroimaging caution and the attention-task caution seem related. Colorful brain results and cleaner reaction-time scores can both be interesting, but neither should be treated as a shortcut around active controls, replication, and careful causal language.

  • Self-Processing and Integrated Conscious Experience

    Score: 21

    The most interesting tension is measurement. S-ART points toward longitudinal work on practice quality, dose, and adverse effects, while IIT has the formal elegance of Φ but faces the problem of applying it to real thalamocortical dynamics.

  • Information Integration Theory of Consciousness

    Score: 23

    The article's substrate-neutral implications feel bold but not indiscriminate. It does not say every information processor is conscious; it asks for architectures that combine specialization with reciprocal integration at the right scale.

  • Mindfulness Training Modifies Subsystems of Attention

    Score: 22

    That caveat also matters for the conflict monitoring result. The experienced meditators looked different at baseline, but by the second test the group difference disappeared, which makes the repeated Attention Network Test exposure hard to ignore.

  • Alpha Oscillations Suppress Distracting Sensory Inputs

    Score: 23

    The retinotopic angle makes the claim feel more concrete to me. Alpha increasing over cortex that corresponds to the ignored hemifield is a cleaner prediction than a vague statement that attention changes oscillations somewhere in the back of the head.

  • Mindfulness Research Needs Less Hype and More Rigor

    Score: 21

    One thing I liked is the neuroscience caution. Brain-imaging findings can be useful for hypotheses, but the paper is right to resist turning group averages and colorful figures into simple stories about causality or clinical improvement.

  • Mindfulness as Self-Awareness, Regulation, and Transcendence

    Score: 20

    I am glad the review keeps adverse effects in view. Intensive contemplative practice can be discussed with too much therapeutic certainty, so the call for better measures of practice quality and expertise seems just as important as the neural model.

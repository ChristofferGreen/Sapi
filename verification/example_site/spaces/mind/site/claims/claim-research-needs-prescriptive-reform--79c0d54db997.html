Research needs reform

Claim Statement

Future mindfulness research should use precise terminology, stronger construct measures, active controls, preregistration, adverse-event monitoring, detailed intervention reporting, and cautious public communication.

Primary source: Mindfulness Research Needs Less Hype and More Rigor

Overview and Interpretation

The discussion of questionnaires shows why simple mindfulness scores can mislead. The listed measures differ in origins and factor structures, and the authors highlight problems with introspection, social desirability, demand characteristics, and different item meanings before versus after training. The recommended path is to measure more specific cognitive, affective, behavioral, and biological capacities rather than treating mindfulness as a unitary latent trait. The practical lesson is reform rather than rejection. The authors want mindfulness research to adopt clearer taxonomies, stronger reporting checklists, active control groups, preregistration, multidisciplinary and skeptical collaboration, adverse-event assessment, and careful public communication.

Three Self-Processing Systems

Evidence Excerpt

The networks identified in the S-ART framework elaborates upon past conceptualizations of self and distinguishes between two functional aspects of self-specifying processes, (1) non-conscious sensory-affective-motor processing, referred to here as the experiential enactive self (EES); (2) an agentic, self-as-subject acting as awareness in the present moment, referred to as an experiential phenomenological self (EPS). The S-ART also distinguishes between self-specifying processes and self-related processes, which refer to the evaluative self-as-object, reflecting the autobiographical narrative reconstructed from the past or projected into the future.

Type: formal_argument

The excerpt provides the taxonomic structure that lets the paper connect phenomenology to neural systems. EES, EPS, and narrative self-processing are not presented as a complete theory of self, but as a practical parcellation for identifying where mindfulness training might change perception, agency, and autobiographical evaluation.

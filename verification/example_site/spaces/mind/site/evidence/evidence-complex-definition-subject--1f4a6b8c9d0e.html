Complexes Define Experiential Loci

Evidence Excerpt

A complex is a subset S having Φ>0 that is not included within a larger subset having higher Φ. For a complex, and only for a complex, it is appropriate to say that, when it enters a particular state out of its repertoire, it generates an amount of integrated information corresponding to its Φ value. Complexes are the “subjects” of experience, being the locus where information can be integrated.

Page references: p.6

Type: formal_argument

The complex definition gives the theory its boundary rule. Elements outside a complex may exchange signals with it, but they do not belong to the same subject of experience unless they raise the integrated-information structure. This supports later claims about afferents, efferents, reticular systems, and subcortical loops influencing consciousness without directly constituting it.

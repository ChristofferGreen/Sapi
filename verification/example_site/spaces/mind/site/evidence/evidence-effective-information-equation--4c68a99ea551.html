Effective Information Equation

Evidence Excerpt

Specifically, we define the effective information between A and B as EI(A→B) = MI(AHmax;B). Here MI(A;B) = H(A) + H(B) - H(AB) stands for mutual information, a measure of the entropy or information shared between a source (A) and a target (B). For a given bipartition of a subset, the sum of the effective information for both directions is indicated as EI(A ↔ B) = EI(A→B) + EI(B→A).

Page references: p.4

Type: equation

The equation is the formal hinge between causal perturbation and information integration. It defines effective information as mutual information generated when one part of a system is perturbed with maximum entropy, then combines both directions across a bipartition. This lets the theory distinguish strongly specialized reciprocal causal organization from mere correlation or passive information storage.

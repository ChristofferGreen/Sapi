Modular And Port Architectures

Evidence Excerpt

A cerebellum-like organization with three modules of eight elements yields three separate complexes with low values of Φ (Φ = 20 bits), and a large complex encompassing all the elements has an extremely low Φ value of 5 bits. A reticular element providing common input to a thalamocortical-like main complex has a combined 9-element complex with much lower Φ (Φ = 10 bits), while the thalamocortical-like main complex remains high (Φ = 61 bits). Afferent and efferent pathways can affect the main complex while the larger combined complex has low Φ (Φ = 10 bits).

Page references: Fig.4

Type: figure_result

Figure 4 grounds two linked claims: modular architectures can be information-rich yet poor in integration, and external input/output systems can influence a conscious complex without becoming part of it. The evidence explains why the cerebellum, brainstem arousal systems, sensory pathways, and motor pathways are assigned different constitutive roles despite their causal importance.

Retreat Alerting Contrast

Evidence Excerpt

At Time 2, planned contrasts for alerting found no significant MBSR-control difference, but the collapsed control and MBSR groups differed from the retreat group in RT scores [t(1,49) = 3.55, p < .001]. No-cue RTs were significantly shorter in the younger retreat group than in the control plus MBSR group [t(1,40) = 2.17, p = .03], whereas double-cue RTs were not significantly different.

Page references: p.115, Figure 4

Type: statistical_result

This result supports the interpretation that retreat participation altered alerting-related performance by improving target detection when no temporal or spatial cue was available. The double-cue non-difference narrows the effect to no-cue readiness rather than a general speed advantage across alerting conditions. The evidence is strongest for the younger retreat subgroup when explicit RT comparisons are made because the full retreat group had broader age variation.

Neuroimaging Confound Limits

Evidence Excerpt

The article states that fMRI and other neuroimaging pictures do not convey the complex biological and computational chain behind brain-structure and brain-function inferences, that such inferences often come from group averages, that diverse practitioners are sometimes pooled in aggregate analyses, and that many studies use cross-sectional designs. It also identifies head movement and cardiorespiratory effects as especially notable physical artifacts in meditation neuroimaging.

Page references: pp.50-51

Type: formal_argument

This evidence supports a cautious interpretation of contemplative neuroscience. It does not deny that brain differences may exist; it limits what can be inferred about causality, clinical significance, and practice-specific mechanisms from common neuroimaging designs and public-facing brain images.

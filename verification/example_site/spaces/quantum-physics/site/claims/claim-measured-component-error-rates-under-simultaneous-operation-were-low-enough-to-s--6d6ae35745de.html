Gate errors predict fidelity

Claim Statement

Measured component error rates under simultaneous operation were low enough to support a model predicting measurable fidelity for full random circuits.

Primary source: Quantum Supremacy Using a Programmable Superconducting Processor

Overview and Interpretation

This claim can be read as the following statement: Measured component error rates under simultaneous operation were low enough to support a model predicting measurable fidelity for full random circuits.

In this space, the claim is currently referenced by 0 topic page(s) and 1 source page(s).

Pages Using This Claim

  • source Quantum Supremacy Using a Programmable Superconducting Processor

    This Nature article reports Sycamore random-circuit sampling on 53 qubits. Cross-entropy benchmarking, component error calibration, and classical-cost estimates support a task-specific quantum supremacy claim. The result is a benchmark speedup, not general fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Evidence Items

  • Largest Circuit Fidelity Model

    The largest random circuits have 53 qubits, 1,113 single-qubit gates, 430 two-qubit gates, and a measurement on each qubit, for which the authors predict total fidelity of 0.2%.

  • System-Wide Error Rates

    Figure 2 reports average simultaneous-operation errors of single-qubit e1 = 0.16%, two-qubit e2 = 0.62%, two-qubit cycle e2c = 0.93%, and readout er = 3.8%.

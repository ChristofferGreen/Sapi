Elided circuits retain fidelity

Claim Statement

The largest elided 53-qubit, 20-cycle circuits yielded F_XEB = (2.24 ± 0.21) × 10^-3 over 30 million samples, supporting an average fidelity above 0.1% with 5σ confidence.

Primary source: Quantum Supremacy Using a Programmable Superconducting Processor

Overview and Interpretation

This claim can be read as the following statement: The largest elided 53-qubit, 20-cycle circuits yielded F_XEB = (2.24 ± 0.21) × 10^-3 over 30 million samples, supporting an average fidelity above 0.1% with 5σ confidence.

    This Nature article reports Sycamore random-circuit sampling on 53 qubits. Cross-entropy benchmarking, component error calibration, and classical-cost estimates support a task-specific quantum supremacy claim. The result is a benchmark speedup, not general fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Evidence Items

  • Elided-Circuit XEB Result

    For the largest 53-qubit, 20-cycle circuit, N_s = 30 × 10^6 samples over ten instances gave F_XEB = (2.24 ± 0.21) × 10^-3, implying average fidelity above 0.1% with 5σ confidence.

  • Verification Circuit Agreement

    The predicted, patch and elided fidelities all show good agreement with the fidelities of the corresponding full circuits, despite vast differences in computational complexity and entanglement.

  • Cross-Entropy Fidelity Formula

    F_XEB = 2^n <P(x_i)>_i - 1, where P(x_i) is the ideal-circuit probability of each measured bitstring and the average is taken over observed bitstrings.

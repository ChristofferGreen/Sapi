WCC therefore integrates the aiT static WCET analyzer into the compiler, translates low-level compiler IR into aiT’s CRL2 format, invokes aiT transparently, imports timing results, and back-annotates those results to source-level ICD-C structures. This infrastructure supports automated loop-bound analysis, source-level optimizations such as procedure cloning and positioning, and assembly-level optimizations such as scratchpad allocation and WCET-aware register allocation. The evidence base is largely benchmark-driven. The loop analyzer handles 707 loops across 96 benchmarks with 99% analyzability and 96% exactness, while slicing and polyhedral evaluation reduce analysis time sharply on selected examples. Procedure cloning can reduce WCET estimates dramatically when call contexts make loop bounds constant, though the paper is candid about code-size expansion. Procedure positioning improves cache behavior using WCET-derived call frequencies. ILP-based code and data scratchpad allocation reduce WCET by prioritizing regions and variables with high worst-case benefit. Register allocation avoids spill code on the WCEP and gives the largest average reduction among the reported individual optimizations. The final matmult experiment shows that several optimizations can compose, reducing WCET from 775,720 cycles to 227,794 cycles. The main limitation is that the results are tied to the WCC/TriCore/aiT environment and to benchmark suites, but the paper provides a coherent architecture and quantitative evidence for WCET-aware compilation as a system-level approach.",

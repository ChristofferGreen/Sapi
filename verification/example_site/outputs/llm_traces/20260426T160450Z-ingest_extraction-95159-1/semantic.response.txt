    "summary_long": "Kimble's source treats the quantum internet not as a near-term communication product but as a research program for connecting quantum systems without destroying their coherence. The central architectural idea is that quantum information must be generated, processed, and stored locally in nodes, then moved or entangled across channels by interfaces that reversibly translate states between matter and light. This distinguishes a fully quantum network from merely classically coordinated quantum processors: when channels preserve quantum coherence, the accessible state space scales across all nodes rather than remaining a sum of separate node spaces. The physical burden then falls on light-matter interfaces. In cavity QED, single atoms inside high-finesse resonators can reach strong coupling, allowing a single photon and a single atom to interact coherently; dark-state adiabatic protocols show how a photonic qubit can be absorbed into an atomic memory and later re-emitted.
